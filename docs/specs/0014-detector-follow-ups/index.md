# 0014. Detector follow ups

**Date**: 2026-10-14
**Status**: Proposed

## Summary

Feature 12 and its four reviews left a family of misses in spec 0005: a neighbour's digit group, or a footnote marker glued to a value, hides the value or gives it the wrong row. This spec settles them in one pass. The phone detector learns to rescue a digit group it would otherwise strand beside a number (today `9346 212 555 0123` lists `9346 212 555` and leaves `0123` in the file). The find step learns that a small glyph raised or lowered off its line (a superscript, a subscript, an ordinal's letters) is not part of the text a detector reads, so a footnote marker can never glue to a value of any kind, and a row can never hold one, which today refuses the whole run. The one digit text rules feature 12 added stay for text that was read by OCR (optical character recognition, where a marker comes back as a plain digit), and gain the three cases the fourth review measured. Everything was measured on real MuPDF, on a page printed by Chromium, and on the 1,235 strings the detector tests and fixtures hold before it was chosen; the figures are in `rationale.md`.

## Requirements

**User stories**:
- As a visitor reviewing a document, I want a phone number that has digit groups beside it listed whole, so that no digit of it is left in the file next to a row that looks complete.
- As a visitor, I want a value followed by a footnote marker listed and removable whatever its kind and however the marker is drawn, so that a reference mark never hides an email address or a phone number, and ticking the row never fails the run.
- As the maintainer, I want the premise that MuPDF reads a raised marker as glued held by a test on a real raised glyph, so that an engine upgrade cannot quietly bring the miss back.

**Acceptance criteria**:

- **AC-1**: The phone detector chooses valid windows as today (spec 0005, *Detectors* step 6: at each unit outside a chosen row, the longest valid window starting there), then rescues stranded units in one pass. The valid pass leaves no free unit at which a valid window starts, so every valid window that covers a free unit starts inside a chosen row. For each unit inside a row, in run order, the longest valid window starting there is asked once (the window cache serves it); each stretch of free units that window covers joins the nearest row to its left inside the window, over the whole stretch. A row never takes another row's units, and a second pass joins nothing (pinned). A joined row's `start` is its first unit's `start` (a `+` included), its `end` the last joined unit's end. Then the pass over possible windows runs unchanged over the units still free. So `9346 212 555 0123` lists one row `9346 212 555 0123`; `6293 6608 0005 7382 276 020 7946 0958` lists the card and `020 7946 0958`; `212 555 0123 9346 415 234 5678` lists `212 555 0123` and `9346 415 234 5678`; `Box 2000 212 555 0123` still lists `212 555 0123` alone; `Ste 1 212 555 0123` still lists `1 212 555 0123`; `020 7946 0958 020 7946 0321`, `212 555 0123 212 555 0124` and every other side by side case in spec 0005's AC-27 still list each number alone. Measured against the bars in `rationale.md` (digits of a number left with no row never above today's in any layout, two numbers side by side never sharing a row, and none of the 1,235 test and fixture strings changing), and against spec 0005's AC-16 budget.
- **AC-2**: A row AC-1 joined starts unticked, whatever its windows: no valid window spans it, since the valid pass would have taken that window. Its `reading` is the row's own window's and is never read for a tick. A row no rescue touched keeps today's tick, so `1 212 555 0123` stays ticked and `9346 212 555 0123` and `9346 415 234 5678` start unticked.
- **AC-3**: Two unit rules uphold INV-14 for text with no raised glyph. First, a phone unit whose own digits are a numeric date plus exactly one more digit at its end is a date unit, so no window holds it: a date with a four digit year (`05.12.19801`, `1980-12-051`, and `12.05.1980.1`, a dot then one digit, as INV-17's dot rule reads it), or a date with a two digit year whose day and month both have two digits (`12-05-801`; the latter measured on the corpus in task 7, and mirrored from AC-9). Second, inside a hyphen glued unit, a year first date (four digit year, then two digit month and day, hyphen glued) that does not start the unit becomes its own unit, cut on both sides, and the hyphens at the cuts end the run as a hyphen between units does today: `00-2026-09-28-11` parts into `00`, `2026-09-28` and `11`; `01-02-03-04-05` has no four digit group and is untouched; `2026-09-27-01` starts with its date and is untouched, so it keeps today's row. Through `detect` on NFKC text: `DOB: 05.12.1980¹ 12 High Street` lists the date, ticked, and no phone row holding its digits; `John Smith 05.12.1980¹ 44 Engineer`, `Born 05.12.1980¹ 44 years`, `John Smith 1980-12-05¹ 44`, `Page 3 05.12.1980¹` and `27-09-2026¹ 5` each list the date; `2026-09-27 10:00-2026-09-28 11:00` lists both dates and no phone row; `01-02-03-04-05` still lists an unticked phone row; `12-05-19801` alone lists `12-05-1980` and no phone row.
- **AC-4**: The find step reads a glyph as off its line by geometry, per read and per MuPDF line, never across a line or a block. The line's solid glyphs are its non whitespace characters; whitespace is never off the line. The dominant height is the upper median of the solid glyphs' quad heights, and the dominant baseline the median, over the solid glyphs taller than `OFF_LINE_SIZE_MAX` times the dominant height, of each origin's offset along the line's normal (`(-dy, dx)` from `Character.direction`). When fewer than `OFF_LINE_MIN_LINE_GLYPHS` (3) solid glyphs are that tall, no glyph on the line is off it by geometry. Otherwise a glyph is off the line when its quad height is at most `OFF_LINE_SIZE_MAX` (0.9) of the dominant height and the absolute distance of its origin from the dominant baseline, along the normal, is at least `OFF_LINE_SHIFT_MIN` (0.15) of the dominant height. A run is a maximal sequence of off line glyphs on one line, whitespace between them allowed. A run leaves the page text when it holds at most `OFF_LINE_STRETCH_MAX` (4) solid glyphs and touches a kept glyph on at least one side with no whitespace between; any other run stays as ordinary text. Where a run leaves, one synthetic space stands in its place, mapped to no character (`source` -1) and not listed in `joins`, unless the position before it is whitespace, or the glyph after it is whitespace, or the run opens or closes its line, where the line join stands instead. A marker MuPDF puts on a line of its own is never off that line; it reaches the detectors parted by the ordinary join, as today. The four constants live in `src/engine`, commented as rules about pages, not caps on the visitor. Measured through MuPDF: a raised 8 pt `1` after 12 pt text (0.67 of the height, raised 0.30 of it), Word's superscript (0.65, 0.24), LaTeX's (0.70, 0.25), Chromium's default `<sup>` (0.83, 0.35) and `<sub>` (0.83, 0.23) are each off the line; 10 pt and 8 pt text on the same baseline, the same size raised 4 pt, a 16 pt glyph, and a 9 pt table cell aligned middle beside 12 pt text (0.75, 0.06) are not; and across every fixture in `tests/fixtures` and the local OCR scans, 78,856 glyphs, none is. A unit test holds that no committed fixture but `detect-markers.pdf` has an off line glyph.
- **AC-5**: The find step also reads a glyph as off its line by code point: a character whose raw code point, before NFKC, is in `SUPERSCRIPT_CODE_POINTS` (U+00AA, U+00B2, U+00B3, U+00B9, U+00BA, U+2070 to U+209C, and the raised letters ordinals use: U+02B0 to U+02B8, U+02E0 to U+02E4, U+1D2C to U+1D6A) is off the line whatever its geometry. Such a glyph counts toward the line's medians and joins a run exactly as AC-4's glyphs do, under the same cap and the same gluing rule. A test pins that every member NFKC normalises to a plain letter or digit. So a full size `¹` glyph in a simple font, which NFKC folds to `1` today, is a boundary.
- **AC-6**: Through the engine, on `detect-markers.pdf`, every value named by `DETECT_MARKERS` is listed with its text without the marker, the tick its kind's rule gives on that text, and `after` beginning at the space or the text after the marker, never at the marker. The lines, each in Helvetica 12 pt with the marker as a second text object at 8 pt raised 5 pt, no gap, unless said otherwise: `Mail john@example.com¹ today`, `Tel 020 7946 0958¹ today`, `Call (212) 555-0123¹ today`, `Call +44 20 7946 0958¹ today`, `Born 12/05/1980¹ on file`, `Born 12/05/80¹ on file`, `SSN 123456789¹ on file`, `SSN 123-45-6789¹ on file`, `Card 4111 1111 1111 1111¹ on file`, `IBAN GB82 WEST 1234 5698 7654 32¹ on file`, `NI AB123456¹ on file`, `NI AB123456C¹ on file` (the email, phone numbers, dates, IBAN and both National Insurance numbers ticked; the Social Security numbers and the card ticked, as spec 0005 lists them without a marker); `DOB 27-09-2026¹ 5 Street` (the date ticked, no phone row); `Signed 27ᵗʰ September 2026 here` and `On 1ˢᵗ of May 2026 here` (the letters raised; the dates listed unticked); `Water H₂O then 020 7946 0958` (the `2` lowered 2 pt; the number ticked); `Born 12/05/1980¹⁰ on file` (a two glyph marker); `Born 12/05/1980١ on file` (the marker through the identity font at 8 pt, raised 5 pt); `Born 12/05/1980¹ on file` at a 1.0 pt gap and at a 1.5 pt gap (the second reads with a space, as today); `Born 12/05/1980¹ on file` with the marker as a full size U+00B9 glyph through Helvetica's WinAnsi encoding; `Born 12/05/1980¹ on file` with the marker at 10 pt raised 4.2 pt (Chromium's shape); the whole line at 8 pt with a 5.5 pt marker raised 3 pt; and the guard line `Tel: ` followed by `020 7946 0958` at 8 pt raised 5 pt with a space before it (a small raised value not glued to a kept glyph; the number listed, ticked). `DETECT_MARKERS` names, per row in reading order, its kind, its text, its tick and the first few characters of `after`.
- **AC-7**: A span's target quads are built one per run of kept characters on each line, parted at each off line stretch inside the span, so no target quad contains an off line glyph's centre. Every row AC-6 lists redacts alone and together through `redactDocumentWith`: the run passes the self check and every marker and raised letter survives in the output's text. A ticked row never holds an off line glyph and never covers one, so the refusal today's `27-09-2026¹ 5` phone row meets (`redaction-overreach`, measured) cannot recur for a marker beside or inside a value. The `superscript.pdf` refusal of spec 0004 (a 4 pt glyph over the middle of a match on a line of its own) still holds.
- **AC-8**: Spec 0005's existing footnote fixture lines, which draw each marker as a plain glued digit at the line's size, keep every row they list today, so the text rules still cover OCR text and PDFs that draw a marker as a plain digit.
- **AC-9**: The one digit text tolerances of spec 0005's third update stay, and three join them. ## Decision

**Chosen option**: rescue stranded units in the phone chooser (not a merge of every overlapping window), an off line boundary in the find step by geometry and by code point with each short glued stretch read as a synthetic space and the target quads parted round it, and the text tolerances kept and extended by the three measured cases (reasoning and the other options in `rationale.md`).

- **The rescue, not the merge.** Merging every overlapping valid window, the card's rule, fixes the leak but glues 60% of random US numbers side by side into one unticked row, because a window across two numbers' middles is valid by chance. Rescuing only a unit that would otherwise be stranded keeps every pair apart (0% shared rows) and loses no digit anywhere today keeps one. Its cost is a tick: a UK number followed by a stray four digit group lists unticked, the group inside its row, about 12% of the time (92% ticked today, 80% after), and an unticked joined row removes none of its digits until the visitor ticks it. The runner up, a rescue only by a window at least as long as the row's own number, keeps 88% of those ticks but leaves 12% of US numbers leaking with three groups before (45% today, 6% with the chosen rule); the owner's rule, extra unticked rows are fine and missed values are not, decides it.
- **Geometry and code point, read as a join.** MuPDF keeps a raised glyph on its line, glued up to a 1 pt gap, and reports its rise and size exactly, so a glyph both smaller than its line and shifted off its baseline is a marker with no grammar to maintain. Size alone would drop small print on the same baseline (8 pt text inside 12 pt), and shift alone would wobble on OCR layers; both together flagged none of 78,856 real glyphs and none of a Chromium printed table with a 9 pt cell aligned middle. The size cap is 0.9 because Chromium's default superscript prints at 0.83, and the shift floor 0.15 because that cell sits at 0.06 while every superscript measured sits at 0.23 or more. A full size `¹` glyph has no geometry and only its code point, so the code point set is the second trigger. A short glued run reads as one synthetic space, none beside whitespace, because that is the one reading under which `27ᵗʰ September 2026` reads `27 September 2026` and `1980¹on` reads `1980 on`; dropping it with no space glues the next word, and keeping it as text breaks ordinals. The gluing rule and the cap keep a spaced value or an isolated word drawn small and shifted in the text. The target quads are parted round an off line stretch inside a span, because a line quad spanning the kept characters would still contain the raised letters of `27ᵗʰ` and target validation would then block the row as unsound.
- **Tolerances kept.** A boundary cannot see a marker that OCR returned as a plain digit on the baseline, and the fixtures draw exactly that. The three new tolerances take the fourth review's measured shapes; the bare Social Security one costs what the separated one already does.
- **Masking deferred.** Running lower kinds with a higher kind's positions read as spaces would tick the rescued `020 7946 0958` after a card and settle the card then date case; it changes how `detect` runs every kind and needs its own measurement, so it stays recorded under Follow-up.

**Implementation skills**: none; this is detector and engine code under the project's own rules.

## Feature design

**Data model sketch** (in memory only, nothing stored or sent):
- `Character` (spec 0005) is unchanged: `quad`, `origin` and `direction` already carry what AC-4 reads. `walkCharacters` still discards the size MuPDF reports, because the quad height is the same signal and is what the self check reads (spec 0004, `LINE_HEIGHT_MIN`).
- `pageText` in `src/engine/find.ts` buffers each line's characters before it writes them (lines are contiguous in extraction order), computes the dominant height and baseline, marks each glyph off line by AC-4 or AC-5, finds the runs, and writes each run that leaves as one synthetic space with `source` of -1, not listed in `joins`; a run that stays is written as ordinary characters. `PageText` and `BlockText` keep their shape, and each `Character` the page keeps is mapped as today. The find step keeps, per read, which characters are off line (a `Uint8Array` over `characters`), which `lineQuads` reads to part a span's quads (AC-7).
- `Window` in `src/detect/phone.ts` is unchanged. The chooser keeps a `rows` list of `{ first, last, window }` while it rescues; a joined row becomes a `Window` with the joined `start`, `end` and `last`, `valid` false, and the row's own `reading`.
- `Unit.date` is set by AC-3's first rule as well as `isNumericDate`; the second rule acts in `unitAt`, where a hyphen glues the next group.
- Engine constants, beside `LINE_HEIGHT_MIN` in `src/engine`: `OFF_LINE_SIZE_MAX = 0.9`, `OFF_LINE_SHIFT_MIN = 0.15`, `OFF_LINE_STRETCH_MAX = 4`, `OFF_LINE_MIN_LINE_GLYPHS = 3`, `SUPERSCRIPT_CODE_POINTS` (a frozen `Set` built from the ranges AC-5 names). Detector constants in `src/detect`: none new; the tolerances are rules in `date.ts`, `us-ssn.ts` and `uk-nino.ts`.

**State transitions**: none. The session machine, the open's order and the refusal point are unchanged.

**Key invariants**:
- **INV-1**: No digit a valid phone window covers is left without a row because another window was chosen: a unit a valid window covers either has its own row, lies in a row, or joins one (AC-1). A rescue never takes a unit another row holds, so INV-13's "no unit belongs to two matches" still holds.
- **INV-2**: A joined row is never ticked (AC-2), so the rescue never removes a neighbour's digits by default.
- **INV-3**: A glyph off its line, by geometry or by code point, is never part of a span, a target's text or a target quad (AC-4, AC-5, AC-7), so no detector meets a real raised marker, the self check never meets one inside a ticked row, and removal never reaches one.
- **INV-4**: The boundary only removes positions from the page text; it never adds a character and never widens the text a span can cover. A run longer than `OFF_LINE_STRETCH_MAX` solid glyphs, or one not glued to a kept glyph, stays, so a spaced value or an isolated word drawn small and shifted is never dropped from detection.
- **INV-5**: A text tolerance drops nothing: it can only list a value that a glued digit hid, with the digit outside its row (spec 0005, INV-17).
- **INV-6**: Every rule here is measured before it lands, and the figures live in `rationale.md`; a rule whose cost on random codes is not recorded is not built.

**Security model**: unchanged. An anonymous visitor, one tab, no server call, the same for every tier. The boundary reads geometry inside the worker only, and nothing new crosses the boundary. The constants are rules about pages, not caps on the visitor, so they live in `src/engine` and `src/detect` where no environment variable can switch a check off (spec 0004, spec 0005, spec 0006, INV-7).

**Configuration required**: none.

## Consequences

**Positive**:
- A phone number beside a stray digit group is never cut to leave part of itself in the file: one random group before a US number left digits with no row 36% of the time, three groups 45%; after the rescue 1.6% and 5.8%, and a UK number after a spaced card is listed whole.
- A footnote marker drawn the way word processors, typesetters and browsers draw one can no longer hide an email address, a phone number, a date with a two digit year, a bare Social Security number or a National Insurance number, and no row ever holds or covers the marker, so ticking a marked value never refuses the run. On the Chromium printed page in `rationale.md`, six of the eleven marked values give no row today.
- The boundary is one rule in one place for every kind, present and future, with no grammar to extend; the per kind text tolerances stay only for text that has no geometry.
- A real raised marker through MuPDF is held by a committed fixture and a test, so an engine upgrade that reads raised glyphs differently fails a test instead of bringing the miss back in silence.
- The INV-14 unit rules close the last two layouts where a numeric date reached a phone row, and the ISO interval's second date lists under Dates.

**Negative / tradeoffs**:
- A UK number followed by a stray four digit group lists unticked with the group inside its row about 12% of the time (92% ticked today, 80% after), and a US number 0.4% with two groups after; the visitor ticks it. Measured on 3,000 random layouts per shape.
- An unticked joined row removes none of its digits by default: for `9346 212 555 0123` today's row removes seven of the number's ten digits and leaves three, the new row removes none until ticked. Nothing is hidden, and the row shows every digit, but a visitor who ticks nothing keeps the whole number where today they kept part of it.
- A joined row's text can hold a neighbour's digits (`9346 212 555 0123`), as a card's row can, so the row reads wider than the number.
- A glyph smaller than its line but on its baseline (small print glued to a value) is on the line for detection and off it for the self check's height rule (`LINE_HEIGHT_MIN`, 0.67 of the target quad's height), so a ticked row holding one refuses the run, as today. The boundary does not change this; recorded.
- A whole value drawn small and shifted, glued to a kept glyph, of up to four solid glyphs would leave the text; a spaced or longer one, or one not glued, stays. No fixture or scan shows the glued shape; the cap and the gluing rule are guards, not measurements.
- The evidence is this project's own fixtures (mostly Helvetica at one size), thirteen simulated scans from one OCR pipeline, and one Chromium printed page. Word, LibreOffice and LaTeX output and other OCR producers were not measured; the shapes they draw were reproduced from their documented sizes and rises. A producer that sizes each word from its box and sits baselines at the box bottom could meet the rule; the fixture test and the scratch flag count are the guards.
- The two digit year tolerance lists an unticked date on 6.4% to 6.7% of random `NN/NN/NNN`, `NN-NN-NNN` and `NN.NN.NNN` codes; the bare Social Security one on 89% of random ten digit runs after an SSN word; the National Insurance one is measured in task 8. The two digit year date unit in the phone detector (AC-3) keeps such a unit out of every phone window; its effect on the corpus is measured in task 7.
- Context lines no longer show a marker that stood after a value, since it left the page text; a visitor sees the space or the words after it.
- A row whose quads are parted round a raised ordinal is drawn as two black boxes with the ordinal's letters left between them.
- The find step buffers one line at a time and reads each line's glyphs twice; on `detect-dense.pdf` this is within the open budget spec 0012's AC-24 holds, to be confirmed by the speed project.
- Masking lower kinds by a higher kind's positions stays undone: the rescued number after a card lists unticked, and `5500 0000 0000 0004 05/12/2026` still lists a phone piece and a date piece. Through `detect` on OCR text, `SSN 223-45-6789¹` still leaves the ticked phone piece spec 0005 pins.

**Neutral**:
- `Character`, `FoundMatch`, `ReviewMatch`, `DetectInput` and the protocol are unchanged; the detectors' tests still build glued text by NFKC, because that is what reaches them from OCR text.
- The 2 s phone budget stays until the CI figure says otherwise.
- `detect-markers.pdf` is a new committed fixture; the existing footnote lines keep drawing a plain glued digit on purpose.
- The CI workflow gains one read only step; `tests/unit/workflows.test.ts` still holds that only the tag deploy writes.
