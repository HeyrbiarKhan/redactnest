## Summary

RedactNest finds sensitive values in a document with small rule based detectors (a pattern for each kind plus its own check, such as the Luhn checksum for card numbers), run inside the worker on the same prepared page the engine redacts. Each find becomes one row in a checklist on `/tool`, with the text around it, so the visitor can judge it and decide what to remove. A row the engine could not remove safely is still shown, but it cannot be ticked and says why. Release 1 builds email addresses and phone numbers; this spec defines all seven kinds, so release 3 (feature 12) builds the other five straight from it.

## Amends specs 0002, 0003 and 0004

- **Spec 0002.** `ReviewMatch` gains one field, `blocked`, and the session reducer refuses to tick a blocked match. `DetectorKind`'s members are decided here, as 0002 asked. `LoggablePayload` gains `DetectionCounts`.
- **Spec 0003.** Feature 6, not feature 8, first places `Checkbox`, `ChecklistGroup`, `ChecklistItem` and `EmptyState` on `/tool`, as a thin checklist, and extends `design-system.spec.ts` to the review state. `Checkbox` gains `disabled` and `ChecklistItem` gains `blockedReason`. Feature 8 still owns the final layout, select all and the summary panel. The kind to label, icon and noun map is owned here, as 0003 asked.
- **Spec 0004.** The three checks inside `validateTargets` are split into predicates that answer per target, so detection and redaction ask the same questions and cannot disagree (INV-3). `validateTargets` keeps its behaviour and its order of kinds. `walkCharacters` in `src/engine/characters.ts` becomes the one character reader for detection too, and learns to repair code points above U+FFFF, which MuPDF.js 1.28.1's walker cuts to 16 bits (AC-26); for every other character the record, the check and validation see exactly what they saw before. Its Follow-ups addressed to feature 6 (`kind` and `text` on every target, both extraction modes, marking slanted and image matches) are met by this spec.

## Requirements

**User stories**:
- As a visitor reviewing a document, I want every email address and phone number it contains listed with the text around it, so I can decide what to remove without reading every page again.
- As a visitor, I want nothing removed unless its box is ticked, and to see and change every tick before I press Redact.
- As a visitor, I want to be told when something was found but cannot be removed safely, so I never believe a value is gone when it is still in the file.
- As a visitor, I want to know what RedactNest looked for, so a short list does not read as a complete redaction.

**Acceptance criteria** (release 1, feature 6):

- **AC-1**: On every page that holds a readable character (spec 0006, AC-3), detection finds each email address in the common form (rules under *Detectors*), in any script. Text after a `mailto:` prefix is found without the prefix, and a sentence's closing full stop is not part of the match. Each is one match of type `email`, ticked by default.
- **AC-2**: Detection finds each phone number that libphonenumber-js, with its `max` metadata, reads as at least possible (the right length for its region) under the reading `PHONE_READINGS` gives it: written internationally (a `+`, or `00` or `011` at the start of its first group, then a country code) or in UK or US national format, where a UK national number is written with its leading `0` or with its country code `44` (the trunk rule). A candidate joined to a longer run of letters and digits (the `2026-000123` inside `INV-2026-000123`) is not a match. Dates, postcodes, ZIP codes including ZIP+4 (`90210-1234`), sort codes, timestamps and Social Security shaped numbers (`123-45-6789`) are not matched, and a full numeric date (`05.12.1980`, `01-02-2003`, `2026-09-27`) is never read as a phone number. An extension written after a number with a marker in `EXTENSION_MARKERS` (`020 7946 0958 ext. 123`) is part of its match; a comma or semicolon never starts one. Each is one match of type `phone`, ticked by default only under AC-10's phone rule.
- **AC-3**: Every occurrence is its own match, even when the same value repeats. `findMatches` returns matches by page, then in reading order. The checklist groups them by kind in `DETECTOR_KINDS` order and keeps that order inside each group. Where two kinds' spans overlap, the higher kind in `PRECEDENCE` keeps its span whole, and the lower keeps each stretch of its own span that no higher match holds, trimmed of whitespace at both ends, as a match of its own kind with its own tick; a stretch with no letter or digit is dropped (amended 2026-10-08, INV-16). So no character on a page belongs to two matches, and no character a detector found is left without a row: `Call 020 7946 0958@example.com` lists `020 7946` as a phone match beside the email `0958@example.com`.
- **AC-4**: Lines in the same text block join with one space, so a value that wraps onto the next line of its block is found, with one quad per line. A line that ends right after `@` or `.` is also tried joined to the next with no space, and that join is kept only when the joined text passes the email rules and does not swallow an email the unjoined text already holds. No match crosses from one text block to another. Other wraps (a break mid word, a hyphenated break) are not found, which is a recorded limit.
- **AC-5**: Each match carries `before` and `after`: up to `contextChars` code points either side, from its own page's text in reading order, NFKC normalised (so a ligature reads as its letters), with runs of whitespace collapsed to one space. `contextChars` of 0 gives empty strings. Context never comes from another page.
- **AC-6**: Every match that is not blocked has a worker private target whose `kind` is the match's `type` and whose `text` is the match's raw extracted characters (spec 0004's contract, which refuses a target without them), and that target passes spec 0004's target validation (its AC-27 to AC-29) on its own and together with every other unblocked match in the document. Ticking any set of unblocked matches and pressing Redact never fails with `unsupported`, `slanted-text`, or the `redaction-overreach` of 0004's AC-29, and the ticked text is absent from the output. The self check can still refuse a run for the causes 0004 records as honest limits, which no review time check can see (listed under *Consequences*).
- **AC-7**: On left to right text, a match's quads equal the quads MuPDF's `page.search()` returns for the same text at the same place, corner by corner, within `POSITION_TOLERANCE`. On text whose extraction order runs against its visual order, a match's target passes validation and redacts (AC-6).
- **AC-8**: A match the engine would refuse is listed, and blocked with the first reason that applies, checked in this order: `unsound-outline` (a quad or its padded area is unsound, or the characters inside its quads do not read as its text, which is 0004's AC-27), `replacement-text` (AC-9), `slanted-text` (any quad `isTooSlanted`), `image-overreach` (an image under it alone would be blanked past `BOUNDS_REACH_RATIO`). A blocked match has no target, starts unticked, and cannot be ticked, and a `redact` naming its id fails with `unsupported`.
- **AC-9**: Detection reads every page in both `EXTRACTION_OPTIONS` modes. A match found in ordinary mode is blocked `replacement-text` when the characters whose centres lie inside its quads, in extraction order, NFKC normalised and with whitespace removed, spell a different string with replacement text ignored than in ordinary mode. A match found only with replacement text ignored (glyphs drawn behind unrelated replacement text) is listed, blocked `replacement-text`. No match is ever skipped because it touches replacement text: it is listed, blocked or not. A match inside a replacement span that is wider than the match, and whose string equals its glyphs, cannot be told apart through MuPDF.js 1.28.1 (measured: both modes give the same characters, origins, quads, font and size as for a span wrapping exactly the match, which redacts cleanly), so it is listed unblocked and stays with the engine's refusal (recorded limit).
- **AC-10**: `email` starts ticked; so do `card`, `iban`, `us-ssn` and `uk-nino` in release 3, except a National Insurance number with a letter glued to its end, which starts unticked (AC-23, amended 2026-10-10). A `phone` starts ticked only when it is valid (`isValid()`) and written like a phone number: with a `+`, `00` or `011` country prefix, with a space, hyphen, dot or parentheses inside it, or after a phone word (`phone`, `tel`, `telephone`, `mobile`, `mob`, `cell`, `fax`, `call`, any case, a whole word) that ends within `KEYWORD_REACH` characters before it in the same block. A plausible number that is not valid, and a valid number written as bare digits with no phone word before it (the order number `12345678901`), start unticked. A `date` starts unticked unless a birth word ends within `KEYWORD_REACH` characters before it in the same block, a birth word glued to its start included, directly or through one separator (`DOB27/09/1980`, `DOB-12/05/1980`, `D.O.B.12.05.1980`), since the word's whole word test looks only at what stands before the date (amended 2026-10-10, second update; the third update of 2026-10-11 drops the claim that a glued word needs its own tick). A date label glued to a date (`Date27/09/2026`, `Exp.31.12.2026`) lists it and leaves it unticked, unless a birth word ends within `KEYWORD_REACH` before it as for any date (`Born Date27/09/1980` is ticked). A Social Security number or a card with one footnote digit stuck after it starts unticked (AC-20, AC-22, third update of 2026-10-11). A blocked match always starts unticked.
- **AC-11**: Detection runs inside `open`, after `inspecting`, and reports the `detecting` phase. It reads one page at a time, and after each of a page's three reads (ordinary mode, replacement text ignored, the image pass) it yields a macrotask and checks for a cancel, so a cancel or a replacement open is noticed within one read. A cancelled open posts nothing and keeps nothing.
- **AC-12**: A page that holds a readable character and throws while detection reads it fails the open with `unsupported`. A page with none is not read by detection at all; a stamped scan's stamp is still searched (spec 0006, which reworded this from "a page that reported a text layer").
- **AC-13**: While reviewing, redacting and complete, `/tool` shows one `ChecklistGroup` per kind that has at least one match (icon, label, and a count with its noun), and one `ChecklistItem` per match, in AC-3's order, with its checkbox bound to the session's tick set. Toggling a row dispatches `tick-toggled`. Every checkbox is disabled while a run is under way. A blocked row shows a disabled checkbox and its reason line, and the reason joins the row's accessible description. The checklist sits outside the polite live region. The review state is keyboard reachable with visible focus, and passes axe in a real browser, forced colours included (WCAG 2.2 AA).
- **AC-14**: An info callout always sits above the checklist, naming what was looked for (built from `DETECTOR_KINDS`) and saying that anything else, such as names and addresses, stays in the file. With no matches at all, an `EmptyState` says nothing was found and that Redact still makes a cleaned copy.
- **AC-15**: No network request, browser store or log carries a match's text, its context or anything a detector produced. `src/detect` has no console, network or storage call. The only document strings that cross the boundary are `text`, `before` and `after` (0002, INV-1). `DetectionCounts` (counts by kind and by blocked reason, nothing else) is a `LoggablePayload`, held by the type gate in `tests/unit/loggable.test.ts`.
- **AC-16**: Every detector runs in time linear in its input. A 100,000 character adversarial block for each detector finishes within that detector's budget in the unit project, measured as CPU time: 1 second for `email` and for each release 3 detector, 2 seconds for `phone`, whose every candidate costs one parse by libphonenumber-js. The shapes are long runs of name characters with no `@`, digit runs with every separator, single and two digit groups (`2 `, `0 `, `02 `, and random digits), repeated `00`, `011`, `(0)` and `+` prefixes, real numbers side by side, and repeated partial dates, and (from 2026-10-08) a card beside its expiry, and long runs of four and of six digit groups. On every one of those shapes the phone detector also asks libphonenumber-js for at most `MAX_PARSES_PER_GROUP` (10) parses per digit group, counted as real calls to the parser (after the per block memo, so the random digit shapes, which the memo cannot shorten, are what test the bound) rather than timed, so that bound holds on any machine. No pattern nests a quantifier inside another, or repeats alternatives that can match the same text. This bounds the detectors' own time on a string; the time MuPDF takes to read a page is measured in Follow-up, not bounded here.
- **AC-17**: Only `src/detect` imports `libphonenumber-js`, and only `src/engine` imports `@/detect`, enforced by lint in every zone, `import()` and `typeof import` included. So the library ships in the worker's chunk and never in a page's.
- **AC-18**: This feature closes the browser steps other specs left marked **(after feature 6)**, except the two that need a real scan, and `/check verify` for feature 6 runs each one and ticks it in its own `verify.md`:
  - spec 0002: with a tick changed, choosing a second file asks first, and accepting replaces the session while declining keeps it (its AC-1); with a tick changed, closing the tab brings the browser's leave warning (its AC-13); from `complete`, changing one tick, running again and downloading a second time without the file picker gives a file that matches the new ticks (its AC-14);
  - spec 0004: a ticked match leaves a box no wider than the match and no taller than its line, and pasting the page's text shows the match gone and its neighbours present (its AC-4, AC-6); in single spaced text, the lines above and below read exactly as before (its AC-4, AC-6); two ticks, then one unticked and run again, shows the unticked match in plain text (its AC-11);
  - spec 0003's review vocabulary checks, which waited for the checklist primitives to be placed on a page: Enter and Space on a group summary close and open it, a long unbroken email wraps inside its row, the checkbox falls back to the native control in forced colours, a screen reader reads a row as the match then "Page N" and the context line, and a compact count badge reads its count with its noun (a group reads "12 email addresses").

  Spec 0004's two steps on a real OCRmyPDF scan wait for feature 7 (scanned page detection and warnings), because real scans are its job, and are marked **(after feature 7)** in spec 0004's `verify.md` and in this spec's: on a real scan, a ticked match with descenders leaves no ink around or below its box (its AC-5, AC-13); on a scan fed about a degree crooked, a name redacts and a long line is shown blocked `slanted-text` during review (its AC-28, AC-29).
- **AC-25**: Detection never calls MuPDF's `search()`. It walks every character of every page, so the 500 quad cap in MuPDF.js 1.28.1's search (`max_hits` in `runSearch`, which drops the rest without saying so) can never drop a match. A page holding 600 email addresses gives 600 matches. Wherever a test compares against `search()` (AC-7, the `findTargets` helper), it fails when `search()` returns 500 quads, since that answer may be cut short.
- **AC-26**: No character is lost or changed between the page and a match. Detection reads characters through the same reader as target validation, the character record and the self check (`walkCharacters`). That reader returns every code point whole, including letters above U+FFFF, which MuPDF.js 1.28.1's walker cuts to their low 16 bits (measured: U+1D400 comes back as U+D400, a different letter, and U+1D800 as U+D800, a lone surrogate; U+20BB7 and U+2D800 follow the same arithmetic, to U+0BB7 and U+D800). An email whose local part holds U+20BB7 (𠮷, a character used in Japanese names) and U+2D800 is found with those letters in its `text`, redacts, and passes the self check. Every pattern uses the `u` flag and steps by code point, never by UTF-16 unit.
- **AC-27**: Phone numbers side by side are each found, one match per number, and no match holds a digit of its neighbour. That covers a run of numbers separated only by single spaces (`020 7946 0958 020 7946 0321 020 7946 0123`), a column with one number per line in one text block (which the line join makes the same run), a comma or semicolon list (`020 7946 0958, 020 7946 0321`), a number between other digit groups (`12:30 020 7946 0958 3 min`, `020 7946 0958 2 020 7946 0321`), numbers split by a slash (`020 7946 0958/0959` lists `020 7946 0958`), and a real number beside a look alike (`CA 90210-1234 (212) 555-0123` lists `(212) 555-0123` whole).

**Acceptance criteria** (release 3, built by feature 12 against this spec):

- **AC-19**: `date` finds a full date (day, month and year together) in the forms under *Detectors*, and nothing shorter. It follows AC-10's tick rule. A full, real date is never dropped for what follows it or what it is joined to; at worst it is listed unticked (amended 2026-10-10, second update, INV-17). A date followed by a separator and a word, or glued to a letter, is found: `DOB 12/05/1980-Smith` lists `12/05/1980`, ticked, `Mon 27/09/2026-Tue 28/09/2026` lists both dates, and `05.12.1980a`, `2026-09-27Tuesday`, `2026-09-27T10:00:00Z` and `27 September 2026AD` each list the date alone. A date followed by a different separator and digits is found too (`05.12.1980-17` and `2026-09-27/01` list the date). A birth word glued to a date's start, directly or through one separator, lists it and ticks it: `DOB27/09/1980`, `DOB-12/05/1980`, `D.O.B.12.05.1980`, `Born-05.12.1980` and `DOB27 September 1980` each list the date, ticked, while `Reborn27/09/1980` gives none. A written date that names a range of days is one row holding both days (`3–5 June 2026`, `3rd-5th of June 2026`, `June 3–5, 2026`), and month first the dash may have spaces round it (`June 5 - 7, 2026`); day first, `3 - 5 June 2026` still lists `5 June 2026`, and `Table 2 - 14 March 2026` lists `14 March 2026`. A day range before a numeric date lists that date (`5-7/6/2026` lists `7/6/2026`). The full date in a range across months is found (`28 May-3 June 2026` lists `3 June 2026`, `Dec 30-Jan 2, 2026` lists `Jan 2, 2026`), and so is `REF-27 September 2026`'s date. A footnote marker stuck after a date does not hide it (third update of 2026-10-11): exactly one ASCII digit right after a date whose year has four digits is read as a marker (NFKC reads `¹` as `1`), the date is listed with the digit outside its row, and a birth word still ticks it: `Born 12/05/1980¹`, `Signed 27 September 2026¹`, `Sep 27 2026²`, `1st of May 2026³`, `2026-09-27¹`, `DOB: 12/05/1980¹` and `27/09/2026¹-28/09/2026` (both dates) each list the date alone, and so does a dotted date at a sentence's end with a marker after the full stop (`geboren am 12.05.1980.¹` lists `12.05.1980`). - **AC-20**: `card` finds 13 to 19 digits that pass Luhn and start with a real issuer prefix at a length that issuer uses, including one with other digit groups beside it on its line (AC-28). ## Decision

**Chosen option**: Option 1: rule based detectors in a pure `src/detect` folder, with libphonenumber-js for phone numbers, called by one find step in the engine.

Detection is a set of pure functions from text to spans, one per kind with its own validator, living in a new capability folder with no MuPDF in it. A new engine step reads each prepared page's characters in both extraction modes, hands each text block to the detectors, turns every span into quads with the same geometry `page.search()` gives, and blocks any match the engine's own target validation would refuse.

Decided within it (the runner up in brackets):

- **libphonenumber-js 1.13.14 with `max` metadata judges; our own scanner finds the candidates.** The phone detector cuts candidate windows out of each run of digit groups itself, and asks `parsePhoneNumberFromString(window, { defaultCountry, extract: false })` from `libphonenumber-js/max` about each window at most once: `isPossible()` lists it, and `isValid()`, which checks digits only with `max` metadata, is what AC-10's tick needs. `extract: false` makes the parser judge the whole window rather than hunt inside it, and it is a declared option, so no undeclared type is needed. The worker already carries the MuPDF WebAssembly binary, so the 157 KB of `max` metadata (measured, before compression) costs little (basis: libphonenumber-js's README on `min` and `max`). (`findNumbers` with `leniency: "POSSIBLE"`, which this spec first chose and slices 2 to 4 built: measured on 2026-09-27, it finds nothing in a run of spaced numbers or around a number with another digit group beside it, reads the comma in `020 7946 0958, 020 7946 0321` as an extension and so swallows the next number's area code, and takes about a second per region on 100,000 adversarial characters. Evidence in `rationale.md`.)
- **Valid windows first, then possible ones.** Inside a run, the leftmost longest window that is valid is taken first, across the whole run, and scanning resumes after it; then the leftmost longest possible window fills each stretch that is still free. So `Box 2000 212 555 0123` lists `212 555 0123`, not `2000 212 555` (possible, not valid, 10 digits) with `0123` left in the file. (Leftmost longest possible in one pass, which is that failure; or choosing the tiling of the whole run that covers the most digits with valid numbers, which is harder to state and to test.)
- **One reading per window, chosen by how it is written** (`PHONE_READINGS`, below): a window's prefix and digit count pick the one region, or none, it is parsed under. Readings replace running two default regions over every block and merging the results, and a window no reading accepts is never parsed. `PHONE_REGIONS = ["GB", "US"]` stays as the regions the readings name; its order no longer breaks ties, because no window has two readings at one digit count.
- **The trunk rule.** A UK national reading needs the number written with its leading `0` or with its country code `44` (as `+44`, `0044` or `44`). GB metadata accepts national numbers of 7, 9 and 10 digits, so without the rule every bare 7, 9 or 10 digit run is a possible UK number: `90210-1234`, `123-45-6789`, `555-0123` and invoice numbers were all listed as possible GB numbers (measured). Nobody in the UK writes a national number without its `0`. (Excluding named shapes only, which leaves the Social Security shape and bare digit IDs listed.)
- **A full numeric date is never a phone number**, by one predicate, `isNumericDate` in `src/detect/dates.ts`, which release 3's `date` detector reuses for its numeric forms. A unit (digit groups glued by single hyphens or dots) that reads as a full date is never part of a phone window. The trunk rule alone cannot catch a date that starts with `0` (`05.12.1980` reads as `0` then 7 digits, a possible UK number). Because detection and the date detector ask the same predicate, a date is always left for `date` in release 3, whatever `PRECEDENCE` says. (Moving `date` above `phone` in `PRECEDENCE`, which fixes release 3 only and lists dotted dates as phone numbers until then.)
- **A slash separates numbers and never sits inside one.** UK and US numbers never hold a slash, and libphonenumber splits there too, so `020 7946 0958/0959` lists `020 7946 0958` (the shorthand `0959` stays, as a recorded limit). A slash that touches a letter still cuts, so `AB/2026/000123` gives nothing. (A slash gluing groups into one code, as slices 2 to 4 built, which misses the first number.)
- **Extensions come from a fixed marker list**, `EXTENSION_MARKERS`: `ext`, `ext.`, `extn`, `extension`, `x` and `#`, in any case, then 1 to 6 digits (spaces allowed around a word marker; `x` and `#` with their digits flush, *Detectors* step 7). A comma or semicolon, which libphonenumber reads as an auto dial extension, is never a marker, so the comma list bug cannot return. (The library's own marker set, which includes the comma.)
- **The phone budget is 2 seconds, and every way of grouping a number is kept.** Each start costs at most one parse per digit count its readings accept, and a window's verdict is kept for the block, keyed by the reading and the digits parsed, so a table repeating one number parses it once. Measured on a prototype of this design: 0.55 s at worst over 19 adversarial shapes, against about 2.1 s for `findNumbers` over both regions. A test counts parses as well as timing them. (A rule that a national number's first group has at least three digits, which measured 0.36 s and would keep phone under 1 second, at the cost of groupings such as `02 0794 60958` and `0 20 7946 0958`.)
- **Validity alone does not tick a phone number.** Under the US reading, bare digit runs such as the order number `12345678901` and the tail of `INV-2026-000123` are valid numbers (measured). So a candidate joined to a longer run of letters and digits is dropped, and a valid number is ticked only when it is written like one (AC-10). Everything else found is listed unticked, never hidden. (Ticking every valid number, which removes order numbers by default.)
- **Scanners, not one big pattern, where a pattern could backtrack.** Email is found by scanning outward from each `@` over the characters a local part and a domain may hold, then checking the result, which is linear by construction (basis: OWASP, Regular expression Denial of Service). Phone candidates are found by one pass over the block's code points, which builds runs of digit groups; each run is cut into windows of at most `MAX_WINDOW_DIGITS` digits. (One regular expression over the block, which is where email patterns classically blow up.)
- **One character reader, repaired.** Detection reads through `walkCharacters`, extended to report block and line boundaries and each line's `direction`, so detection, target validation, the record and the self check can never disagree about what a character is (basis: spec 0004, INV-12). MuPDF.js 1.28.1 builds each walked character with `String.fromCharCode`, which keeps a code point's low 16 bits only, while `asText()` and `asJSON()` return whole code points (measured). So for each read whose `asText()` holds a code point above U+FFFF, the reader takes each text line's `text` from `asJSON()` and puts its code points in place of the walked characters, one for one. When a line's code point count differs from its walked characters, or a walked character is not its code point's low 16 bits, the read fails closed: `unsupported` in detection and validation, `redaction-incomplete` in the self check (spec 0004's rule for a check that cannot finish). A page with no such code point skips the JSON, so the common case costs one `asText()`. (Reading through `asJSON()` on every page, which works too and costs a serialisation per read; or leaving the walker as it is, which turns 𠮷 into a Tamil letter in the checklist.)
- **Per character NFKC with an index map.** Each character is normalised on its own, and the page string keeps, for every position, the character it came from. Detection sees `ﬁ` as `fi` and a fullwidth `＠` as `@`; the target keeps the raw characters, because that is what 0004's AC-27 compares (basis: Unicode NFKC normalisation; spec 0004, AC-27). (Matching raw text, which misses a name set with a ligature.)
- **Quads built from characters, along the line's direction.** For each line a match touches, measure how far each of the match's characters sits along that line's `direction` (the one MuPDF gives at `beginLine`), by its quad's centre. The character furthest back gives the line quad's upper left and lower left corners (its own), and the one furthest forward gives the upper right and lower right. On left to right text that is the first character's left corners and the last character's right corners, which is how `page.search()` builds its own (AC-7 proves it; basis: spec 0004's geometry, designed and measured on `page.search()` quads); on text drawn against its extraction order it still covers the match. (Asking `stext.search()` for the match text and picking the hit by position, which breaks when search and the walk disagree about a character.)
- **Detection per text block, context per page.** A match can never join two columns or two table cells; its context can.
- **`PRECEDENCE`**: `email`, `iban`, `card`, `us-ssn`, `uk-nino`, `phone`, `date`. A kind with a checksum or a unique marker outranks a looser digit shape. (Longest span wins, which lets a long date string swallow a phone number.)
- **A card is whole units, chosen from inside its run** (update of 2026-10-08, AC-28). Unbroken digits, and digits glued by hyphens, form one unit that is never cut; units parted by one space are separate, and a card is any window of whole units that passes Luhn and `CARD_BRANDS`. A hyphen or dash next to an expiry or a date (`-12/28`) parts rather than glues, so a card typed against its expiry is found while a licence key stays whole. A window of several units, which only a spaced card makes, has a first unit of 4 to 6 digits, a last of 1 to 6 and every other 3 to 6 (`CARD_GROUP_DIGITS`), as cards are printed and as payment forms group them, so a row number, a lone digit or an unbroken card's neighbour never joins a card. A one or two digit group glued to a slash (the `12` of `12/28`) ends the run, so it is never part of a card. From each start the card is the longest window that passes, and every window that passes and starts inside a card's row, when it reaches further, carries the row to its end (update of 2026-10-10): where two readings of a run disagree about where a card starts or ends, the row covers both. Every card starts ticked. Measured on random valid cards of six brands, no card digit is left without a card row in any of seventeen neighbour layouts, a number before the card and more digits after a long one included; the cost is a neighbour's digits inside a card's row in 0.6% to 8.3% of layouts with digits after the card, the rest of that neighbour keeping its own row (AC-3, INV-16). (The longest window whose end is clear, else the shortest, as built on 2026-10-08, which takes a group after a card only when that group ends the run, but left a real card's first or last group with no row in 1.0% to 4.0% of those layouts; or joining only the windows of later starts, the review's narrower fix, which closes the number before a card but still leaves a long card's last group 1.0% to 1.7% of the time. Evidence in `rationale.md`.)
- **Where a higher match cuts into a lower one, the lower keeps the rest as its own row** (update of 2026-10-08, AC-3, INV-16). `detect` keeps each stretch of a lower kind's span that no kept span holds, trimmed of whitespace, with the lower kind and its tick, and drops a stretch with no letter or digit. The higher row is never touched, and each piece is ticked or unticked on its own, so unticking a false card can never untick the rest of a real Social Security or phone number. Measured: a false card that cut a spaced SSN left its other digits with no row in 1.7% to 3.6% of random layouts; with pieces, 0%. On every detection test string and fixture line, the one result that changes is the test that pinned the old rule (`Call 020 7946 0958@example.com`). (Dropping the lower span whole, as first built, which leaves the rest of a real value with no row; or merging both into the higher match, which removes the same digits but relabels a phone number as part of an email or a card, couples their ticks, and would read a line join inside the phone part as nothing under the email's rejoin rule.)
- **A full date is never dropped for what follows it or what it is joined to** (second update of 2026-10-10, AC-19, INV-17, replacing the list of forms the first update added one by one). A date that passes `isRealDate` is listed, ticked or not under AC-10, whatever stands after it: a letter glued to it, a separator and a word, a different separator and digits, a time. Only two shapes drop it, each kept because measurement shows the rule earns its place (evidence in `rationale.md`): a *letter* glued to its start or standing before a hyphen or a slash before it, which is how a reference code is written (`REF-05.12.1980`, `INV-`, `Smith-12/05/1980`; without the rule every such code and every dated file name such as `report-27-09-2026-v2.pdf` lists a row); and its *own* separator running on with a digit on either side, which is how a longer number of the same shape reads (`2026-09-27-01`, `1/05/12/1980`, `05.12.1980.17`; without the rule 62% of random version numbers such as `1.2.3.4`, 35% of French phone numbers such as `01-02-03-04-05`, 9% of sort codes with a fourth group and 15% to 27% of serials of two digit groups list a date). A letter before a *dot* no longer counts (third update of 2026-10-11): a full stop after a letter is how an abbreviation (`Exp.`, `w.e.f.`, `No.`) or a sentence ends, never how a reference code is glued, and the one code shape it admits, a dotted file name (`report.27.09.2026.pdf`), lists an unticked row as `report_2026-09-27.pdf` already does. Two or more digits glued to a date's end still never make a date; exactly one is a footnote marker, the bullet below. Both rejections have three exemptions, the joiner bullet, the birth word bullet and the date label in it. (Keeping the old rule, any separator touching a letter or digit cuts, which dropped every case the second review found; or no rejection at all, which lists the codes above.)
- **A joiner between two full dates parts them, and only a time may stand between** (update of 2026-10-10, AC-19, INV-17, generalised by the second update). A hyphen, dash or slash (never a dot) is a joiner. Right after a date, both rejections are lifted when a full date in any form `date` reads starts right after the joiner. Right before a date, both rejections are lifted when nothing, or only a time, stands between the end of the last date the scan listed and the joiner: a stretch of at most `TIME_LONGEST` (40) code points that holds at least one digit and nothing but digits, whitespace, `:`, `.`, `,`, `+`, `@`, hyphens and letters in runs of at most `TIME_LETTERS_MOST` (5). That is what a time looks like in any form (`10:00`, `, 10:00`, `at 10`, `@ 10:00`, `10h00`, `T10:00:00.123Z`, `10:00 PM EST`, `UTC+1`, `GMT+01:00`, `+0100`), and it needs no grammar of times, so no form is left out and nothing is parsed that could swallow part of the next date. Each date is its own row, ticked or not under AC-10 on its own. `2026-09-27-01` and `1/05/12/1980` stay rejected, because what lies beyond the joiner is not a full date and no date was listed before; `2026-09-27 10:00 Monday-2026-09-28` and `2026-09-27 ref INV-2026-09-28` list the first date only, because a six letter word and a word with no digit are not times. (A grammar of times stepped over after each date, the cross check's first draft, which read `-12` as an hour offset in `27-09-2026 10:00-12-10-2026` and so dropped the second date, and which left `@`, `10h00`, `UTC+1` and `PM EST` outside it; treating every hyphen beside digits as a range, which lists `2026-09-27` out of `2026-09-27-01`; or one row for the whole range, which reads two values as one and couples their ticks.)
- **A written date may name a range of days, and month first the dash may be spaced** (update of 2026-10-10, widened by the second update, AC-19): two days joined by one hyphen or dash, each with its optional ordinal and each a real day of that month and year, before the month with no space on either side of the dash (`3–5 June 2026`, `3rd-5th of June 2026`) or after the month with up to `MAX_GAP` whitespace on either side or none (`June 3–5, 2026`, `June 5 - 7, 2026`). One row holds both days, because the first day alone is not a full date. Day first, a spaced dash still reads as before, the second day's date alone (`3 - 5 June 2026` lists `5 June 2026`), because a number, a spaced dash and a date is also how a numbered heading reads (`Table 2 - 14 March 2026`, `Item 12 - 5 June 2026`), and one row for the whole would remove the number with the date; month first, nothing but a day can stand between the month and the dash. (Spaces in every form, this update's first draft, which the cross check showed swallows heading numbers; or listing only the second day's date month first, which finds nothing in `June 5 - 7, 2026`.)
- **A written date is cut only by a letter glued to its start or two or more digits glued to its end** (update of 2026-10-10, from the cross check, narrowed by the second update and again by the third, AC-19, INV-17). A date with a month name in it is never a code, so a separator beside it (`-`, a dash, `/`, `.`) never cuts it, whatever is beyond, and nor does a letter after its year. That finds the full date in a range across months, `28 May-3 June 2026`, `5 June–3 July 2026` and `Dec 30-Jan 2, 2026`, where the first half has no year and the joiner rule cannot help, and `27 September 2026AD`. `x27 September 2026` and `27 September 202612` stay rejected; `27 September 20261` is the footnote case below. (The joiner rule alone, which still drops those dates; or a written range form spanning two months, which needs a pattern per pairing and finds nothing the simpler rule misses.)
- **A time after a date stays in the file, and the row is the date alone** (update of 2026-10-10, kept by the second update, AC-19). A year first date followed by `T` and a time (`1980-05-12T00:00:00Z` after `DOB`) is a date of birth like any other, listed without its time, as is any date with a time after it; the time matters only to the joiner rule above, which reads it as a stretch between two dates rather than as a form. (Stepping only past the ISO `T` form with a parsed time, as first built, which the second review showed loses the second date in every other form.)
- **A birth word glued to a date lists it and ticks it** (second update of 2026-10-10, from the cross check, AC-10, AC-19, INV-17). When a word in `BIRTH_WORDS`, with no letter or digit before it, ends exactly where the date starts or exactly before the one separator before it (`DOB27/09/1980`, `DOB-12/05/1980`, `D.O.B.12.05.1980`, `Born-05.12.1980`, `DOB27 September 1980`), both start rejections are lifted and the date starts ticked. Text extraction runs a label into its value at the front as often as at the back, and a date of birth is the most sensitive date there is; the exemption costs nothing, since a birth word before a date is what AC-10 already ticks. `Reborn27/09/1980` still gives none, because `born` there has a letter before it. (Leaving it, which drops exactly the date the detector exists for; or listing it unticked, as a glued National Insurance letter is, which that rule needs only because the number has no checksum.) **A date label glued the same way lists the date, unticked** (third update of 2026-10-11, AC-19, INV-17): a word in `DATE_LABELS` (`date`, `dated`, `dt`, `exp`, `expiry`, `expires`, `expired`, `issued`, `valid`, `from`, `to`, `until`, `on`, `effective`, `signed`), with no letter or digit before it, ending exactly at the date or exactly before the one separator before it, lifts both start rejections exactly as a birth word does, through the same window and the same whole word test (`Date27/09/2026`, `Exp.31.12.2026`, `Date-27/09/2026`, `Dated27 September 2026`). The two start rejections were measured against reference codes and file names, which no label begins; a label run into its value is the same extraction accident as a birth word run into its date, and the only shape it costs is a code whose letters spell a label (`DT-`, `TO-`, `ON-`, `EXP-`: 0.4% of random two letter codes before a hyphen dated date, 0.1% of three letter ones, each an unticked row). The row stays unticked, because a label says the value is a date, not that it is a date of birth. (A list of every label, which no list ends: the glued labels not in `DATE_LABELS` stay a recorded miss under *Consequences*; or lifting the glued letter rule for every word, which lists `Dismay 5 2026`'s `may 5 2026` and every code glued to a date.)
- **A footnote marker stuck after a value does not hide it** (third update of 2026-10-11, AC-19, AC-20, AC-22, INV-17). NFKC turns a superscript `¹ ² ³` into `1 2 3`, and MuPDF reads a reference mark set flush after a value as glued to it (measured in the third review: at 8 point, drawn at a gap of 1 point or less after `Born 12/05/1980` it comes back as `19801`), so the value then ended in one digit too many and gave no row, the one case where the second update's rule ("a full real date is never dropped for what follows it") was not kept. Now exactly one ASCII digit stuck after a value, with no digit after it, is read as a marker, and the value is listed with the digit outside its row. For a date, the year must have four digits: a numeric date's last group is read as up to five digits and, when the whole is no date, tried without its last digit (`27/09/20261` reads `27/09/2026`, `2026-09-271` reads `2026-09-27`); a written date's year may have one digit touching it (`27 September 20261`); and a dotted date's own full stop followed by one digit is a sentence end and a marker (`12.05.1980.1`), while a hyphen or a slash and one digit stay the build number shape (`2026-09-27-1`). The date keeps AC-10's tick, so `Born 12/05/1980¹` is ticked. For a separated Social Security number, one digit after its last part lists it unticked (`123-45-6789¹`); a bare run stays nine digits, since only an SSN word and its length say what it is. For a card, a window whose last unit ends in a group of two or more digits and passes once that group's last digit is left out is *tolerated*: at a start where nothing passes it is the row, ending before that digit, and it carries a row the way a passing window does, so a card that a chance window overlaps is still whole (`2226 4111 1111 1111 1111¹` is one row, every card digit in it). A row is ticked when any window inside it passes whole, so a card with a marker is unticked and a card with a number before it stays ticked (the cross check found the first draft, which ticked by the start's window alone, turned `4521 4111 1111 1111 1111` into an unticked row 1.9% of the time). Measured on random codes, the cost is small and every extra row is unticked: `N/N/NNNNN` lists a date 1.9% of the time, `20NN-NN-NNN` 3.2%, the other glued digit shapes 0.0% to 0.2%, a 17 digit number lists an unticked card 2.5% of the time on top of the 0.7% listed ticked today, a 20 digit one 1.6%, a licence key of five four digit groups 1.8%, and a 3, 2 and 5 digit number lists an unticked Social Security number 89% of the time, the SSA's own pass rate on random digits, a shape nothing else writes. (Two or more glued digits, which lists `on 05 12 1980`'s neighbours and tracking numbers, and reads a six digit group as a date and a digit; or dropping the value, as before, which is the silent miss. A National Insurance number with no suffix and a marker, `AB123456¹`, stays the recorded miss: not measured here, under Follow-up.)
- **An IBAN may touch what follows it, never what comes before it** (update of 2026-10-10, AC-21, INV-17). Its country's exact length and its mod 97 check already say where it ends, so the next word or a footnote marker (which NFKC reads as a digit) no longer hides it, and it stays ticked. Measured: 1.0% of random codes that open like an IBAN and run on past its length list one, the 1 in 97 chance that its check passes. The start rule stays, because a letter or digit before it is how a value reads out of a longer code. (Leaving the end rule, which drops a real IBAN whole for one glued character.)
- **A National Insurance number with a letter glued to its end is listed, unticked** (update of 2026-10-10, AC-23, AC-10, INV-17). With no checksum, two letters, six digits and more letters is as often a product code as a number glued to the next word: measured, 55% of random codes of that shape now list a row, so the row starts unticked and the visitor decides. A digit after its six digits still drops it: allowing one would list `on 05 12 1980` and every tracking number shaped `AB123456789GB` as National Insurance numbers. (Ticked, which removes product codes by default; or dropped, as before, which loses a real number for one glued letter.)
- **A blocked match gets no target.** The worker's existing check (an id with no target is `unsupported`) then refuses it with no new code path (basis: fail closed; spec 0004, INV-6). (A target flagged blocked, which every reader of the map must remember to check.)
- **One shared set of predicates** in `src/engine/targets.ts`, used by detection and by `validateTargets` alike (INV-3; basis: spec 0004, AC-27 to AC-29, and its Follow-ups for feature 6). (Detection writing its own copies of the checks, which drift.)
- **Ids from `crypto.randomUUID()`**, minted in the worker, so an id from a finished session can never name a match in a new one. (A counter per session.)
- **The checklist is its own component**, `src/app/tool/review-checklist.tsx`, rendered by `tool-client` only, outside the live region. Labels, icons, nouns, reason copy and `detectionCounts` live in `src/lib/detectors.ts`, typed as records over `DetectorKind` and `BlockedReason`.
- **The email rejoin is narrower than a plain join** (AC-4): a join after `.` that would swallow an email the unjoined text already holds is dropped, so "Call Bob." at a line's end followed by "smith@example.com" never becomes `Bob.smith@example.com`. The cost is a local part wrapped at a dot (`john.` then `smith@example.com`), which lists as `smith@example.com`.

No community skill shaped this design.

## Feature design

**Data model sketch** (in memory only, nothing is stored):

Crosses the boundary, in `src/worker/protocol.ts`:

| `BLOCKED_REASONS` / `BlockedReason` | `unsound-outline`, `replacement-text`, `slanted-text`, `image-overreach` | new, closed, frozen `as const` |

| `DetectionCounts` | `foundByType: Readonly<Partial<Record<DetectorKind, number>>>` · `blockedByReason: Readonly<Partial<Record<BlockedReason, number>>>` | new; joins `LoggablePayload` |

Stays in the worker, in `src/engine/types.ts`:

|---|---|---|
| `RedactionTarget` | `page` (zero based) · `quads` (one per line) · `start`, `end` (offsets into the ordinary mode page string, end exclusive; not read by the engine yet, kept for feature 13) · `kind` · `text` (the raw characters, a line join as one space) | exists; feature 6 fills it |
| `FoundMatch` | `page` (zero based) · `kind` · `text` · `before` · `after` · `tickedByDefault` · `concealed: Concealment \| null` (spec 0006), and either `{ blocked: null, target: RedactionTarget }` or `{ blocked: BlockedReason, target: null }` | new; a union, so a blocked match cannot carry a target |

Pure, never crosses, in `src/detect`:

| Shape | Fields |
|---|---|
| `DetectInput` | `text: string` (one block, lines joined by one space) · `joins: readonly number[]` (the index of each synthetic join space) |
| `Span` | `kind` · `start` · `end` (offsets into `text`, end exclusive) · `tickedByDefault` |

Relationships: a page has many matches; each match has zero or one target (zero exactly when blocked); each `Span` becomes one match. Rules: no two matches in one mode share a character (AC-3); a match found only with replacement text ignored is dropped when any of its characters' centres lies inside an ordinary mode match's quads, so one place on the page is one row.

**State transitions**: spec 0002's session machine is unchanged. Two guards join it: `tick-toggled` on a blocked match returns the session unchanged, and `seededTicks` leaves out every blocked match, whatever its `tickedByDefault` says. Detection adds one phase inside `opening`: `loading-engine`, `opening`, `inspecting`, `detecting`, then `result`.

**The find step** (`src/engine/find.ts`, called as `OpenDocument.findMatches`), per page that holds a readable character (spec 0006):

1. Read the prepared review page in ordinary mode (`EXTRACTION_OPTIONS[0]`) through `walkCharacters`, with its code point repair (AC-26), and build its page text: each text block's characters in order, NFKC normalised one at a time, lines inside a block joined by one synthetic space (none added when either side is already whitespace), blocks joined by one synthetic space, with an index from every position back to its character (or to nothing, for a synthetic space). Image blocks are skipped.
2. Run `detect` on each block, and resolve overlaps across the page by `PRECEDENCE`.
3. Map each span to its characters, group them by line, and build one quad per line (AC-7). Take `text` for display from the page text, and the target's `text` from the raw code points.
4. Read the page again through `walkCharacters` with replacement text ignored (`EXTRACTION_OPTIONS[1]`), detect the same way, and keep only the matches that no ordinary mode match covers (AC-9).
5. Check every match in AC-8's order, with the shared predicates, and block the first that fails. The image check walks the page's images once and answers for every match at once.
6. Cut `before` and `after` from the page text (AC-5).
7. After each of the three reads (steps 1, 4 and 5), yield a macrotask and stop with `RunCancelled` if `isCancelled()` says so. Both structured texts and the page are destroyed in a `finally` on every path, a cancel included.

Nothing in the find step calls `search()`, so no hit cap applies (AC-25).

**Detectors** (`src/detect`, one file per kind, each a pure `(input: DetectInput) => readonly Span[]`, registered in `DETECTORS: Readonly<Record<DetectorKind, Detector>>`):

- **`email`** (release 1). Scan out from each `@`. Local part: 1 to 64 letters (`\p{L}`), marks (`\p{M}`), digits (`\p{N}`) and `. _ % + -`, not starting or ending with `.`, no `..`. Domain: two or more labels of letters, marks, digits and inner hyphens, separated by single dots, the last label two or more letters, 253 characters at most. Boundaries: no local part character right before it, and no domain character, or `.` followed by one, right after it. So `mailto:a@b.com` gives `a@b.com`, and "write to a@b.com." ends before the stop. Rejoin (AC-4): for each join whose previous character is `@` or `.`, scan again with that one join space treated as invisible (skipped as if absent, never deleted from the string), and keep an email that runs across it, when no email already found overlaps it. Its `start` and `end` index the original block text, and the span contains the join space; the match's display text and its target's `text` leave that one space out. It has two quads.
- **`phone`** (release 1, its finder rebuilt by slice 5). Our scanner proposes windows; libphonenumber-js judges each one. In order:
  1. **Runs.** One pass over the block's code points builds runs, by this grammar:
     - A **group** is one or more digits (`\p{Nd}`), written bare or inside one pair of brackets (`(020)`, `(0)`).
     - A **unit** is one group, then any number of further groups, each joined to the one before by exactly one of: a single hyphen (`-`, U+2010 to U+2015, or U+2212 minus), a single dot, or nothing at all where a bracket closes or opens between them (`(0)20`, `(212)555-0123`). Hyphens and dots may mix inside one unit.
     - A **run** is one unit, then any number of further units, each after a gap of 1 to 3 whitespace characters (`\s`; after NFKC and the line join this is almost always U+0020), or after a single slash. A `+` directly before a run's first unit belongs to that unit, with optional whitespace between (`+44 20`, `+ 44 20`); a `+` anywhere else ends the run and starts a new one.
     - Anything else ends the run: a letter, a comma, a semicolon, a colon, a fourth whitespace character, a second joiner in a row.
  2. **Units that can never be part of a phone number.** A unit that reads as a full numeric date under `isNumericDate` is marked, and no window holds it (AC-2).
  3. **Windows.** From each unit, every run of consecutive units starting there is its own window: one unit, two units, and so on, each looked up in step 4 and, when it has a reading, judged in step 5 on its own. Growth stops at a slash (a window never holds one) and once the window holds more than `MAX_WINDOW_DIGITS` (18) digits. A window must try every length, because a longer window can have no reading while a shorter one is a real number: in `020 7946 0958 2 020 7946 0321`, the four unit window from the first `020` holds 12 digits, which no reading accepts, and the three unit window is the number. A bracketed `(0)` group is left out of a window's digits, both for its count and for the digits parsed in step 5. A window's span runs from its first unit's first character (its `(`, or the run's `+` when the window starts the run) to its last unit's last character (a `)` when that unit ends in one). It must not start or end against a letter or digit, or against a hyphen or dot that itself touches one (the `2026-000123` inside `INV-2026-000123`), or against a slash that touches a letter (`AB/2026/000123`).
  4. **Readings.** `PHONE_READINGS` gives a window its reading from its prefix and its digit count together. A window is checked against every row whose prefix it matches, and the row whose digit counts include the window's count gives the reading; a window no row accepts is never parsed. The `00` and `011` rows test the window's first group; every other row tests the window's digits joined together, with any `+` for the first row. For example, a window starting `0113`, which matches both the `011` and the `0` rows, is read as `GB` at 8, 10 or 11 digits and as `US` at 12 to 18, and at 9 digits is not parsed.

     | Window starts with | Parsed as | Digit counts parsed |
     |---|---|---|
     | `+` | international, no default region | 7 to 15 after the `+` (E.164's range) |
     | a first group starting `00`, the UK's international prefix | `GB` | 9 to 17 |
     | a first group starting `011`, the US's international prefix | `US` | 12 to 18 |
     | `0`, not `00` | `GB` national, with its trunk `0` | 8, 10 or 11 (the `0`, then 7, 9 or 10 digits) |
     | `44` | `GB`, its country code written without `+` | 9, 11 or 12 (`44`, then 7, 9 or 10 digits) |
     | `1`, then 2 to 9 | `US` national, with its trunk `1` | 11 |
     | 2 to 9 | `US` national | 10 |

     The national counts are each region's national number lengths (GB 7, 9 and 10; US 10) plus the prefix written before them, and deliberately leave out the lengths libphonenumber-js accepts as local only (a UK number dialled without its area code), which `isPossible()` alone would let through: nobody writes a local only number with a trunk `0`. No start has two readings at one digit count. A window starting `011` with 10 or 11 digits is a UK number (the 011x areas, such as Leeds `0113`), never a US international call. So each start costs at most `MAX_PARSES_PER_GROUP` (10) parses, reached by a first group starting `011` (3 UK counts, 7 US international ones). Every prefix in the table is written in ASCII digits, so a window written in other decimal digits, which step 1 lets into a run, has a reading only when it starts with `+` (a recorded limit, under *Consequences*).
  5. **Judging.** Each window with a reading is parsed once, as its digits (a bracketed `(0)` left out, and with a `+` for the international reading) through `parsePhoneNumberFromString(digits, { defaultCountry, extract: false })`, and its verdict is kept for the block, keyed by reading and digits, so the same digits under the same reading are never parsed twice. It is **possible** when the parser returns a number and `isPossible()`, and **valid** when also `isValid()`. The table's digit counts decide which windows are parsed; `isPossible()` decides which of those are listed.
  6. **Choosing.** First, across the run from left to right, at each unit the longest of the windows starting there that is valid is taken, and scanning resumes after it. Then, over the units still free, at each unit the longest possible window that holds only free units is taken the same way. No two matches share a unit (INV-13).
  7. **Extensions.** After a taken window, a marker in `EXTENSION_MARKERS` and then 1 to 6 digits, with no letter or digit touching the digits' end, join the match. The word markers `ext`, `ext.`, `extn` and `extension` (any case) may have a space on either side. The one character markers `x` and `#` must come right after the number, after one space or none, with their digits flush after them (`020 7946 0958 x123`, `020 7946 0958 #12`); `020 7946 0958 x 12` does not join. They can still take a short token that is not an extension (`020 7946 0958 x2 monitors` removes `x2` too), which removes two characters too many and never leaves a number behind. The extension never changes the verdict.
  8. **Tick.** A match is ticked by default when it is valid and written like a phone number (AC-10), and unticked otherwise. The tick reads the window alone, never its extension, so `12345678901 ext. 4` stays unticked with no phone word before it.

  Every step is linear in the block: the scan is one pass, each start has a bounded number of windows (at most 18 digits), each window is parsed at most once, and the choosing passes mark units as they are taken.
- **`date`** (release 3). A day, month and year together, in one of these forms: day, month and year as numbers with the same separator (`/`, `-` or `.`) twice, where the year has 4 digits or 2, accepted when either a day first or a month first reading is a real calendar date; year first ISO (`2026-09-27`, `2026/09/27`) (these numeric forms are exactly `isNumericDate` in `src/detect/dates.ts`, which slice 5 builds for the phone detector's date rule); written forms with English month names, full or three letter (plus `Sept`), an optional full stop after an abbreviation, an optional ordinal (`1st`, `2nd`, `3rd`, `27th`), an optional `of`, and an optional comma (`27 September 2026`, `27th of Sept. 2026`, `September 27, 2026`, `Sep 27 2026`). Four digit years run 1900 to 2099. Never a year alone, a month and year, a time, or a relative date. Ticked by default only when `date of birth`, `birth date`, `birthdate`, `DOB`, `D.O.B` or `born` (any case, a whole word) ends within `KEYWORD_REACH` characters before the date's start, in its block.

  Amended 2026-10-10 (AC-19, INV-17), and again by the second update of 2026-10-10, which replaced the boundary rules:
  - **Day ranges.** In all three written forms (day first, day first with `of`, month first), the day may be two days joined by one hyphen (`-`, U+2010 to U+2015, or U+2212), each day with its own optional ordinal (`3–5 June 2026`, `3rd-5th of June 2026`, `3-5th June 2026`, `June 3–5, 2026`, `June 3-5 2026`). In the month first form only, the hyphen may have 0 to `MAX_GAP` whitespace characters on each side, counted separately (`June 5 - 7, 2026`, `June 5 -7, 2026`); in the two day first forms it has none, so `3 - 5 June 2026` reads as before, `5 June 2026` with `3 -` left in the file. The range sits right after the first day's ordinal (and, month first, before the optional comma). Both days must be real days of the month and year under `isRealDate`; their order is not checked, so `5-3 June 2026` is one row too. The row is the whole written date, both days and, month first, the spaces round the dash included. The range is one bounded optional group in each pattern, non capturing so the patterns' existing groups keep their numbers, nested in no other quantifier; `WRITTEN_LONGEST` grows by `2 * MAX_GAP` (six) for the month first spaces, on top of the five the range added. The second day is read back from the month first match by a pattern that allows the same spaces. A numeric date after a day range (`5-7/6/2026`) lists the numeric date alone, `7/6/2026`, by the boundary rule below; `5-` stays in the file.
  - **Boundaries (the general rule).** A full, real date is never dropped for what follows it or what it is joined to. A numeric date is dropped only in these cases, and a written date only in the first and last:
    - a letter or digit glued to its start (`REF05.12.1980`, `x27 September 2026`): a value starting inside a word is the end of a longer code;
    - a letter right before a hyphen or a slash right before a numeric date (`REF-05.12.1980`, `INV-05.12.1980`, `Smith-12/05/1980`, `report-27-09-2026-v2.pdf`, `example.com/2026/09/27/slug`): a reference code or a path. A letter before a *dot* never cuts (third update of 2026-10-11): `Exp.31.12.2026`, `w.e.f.27.09.2026`, `b.12/05/1980` and `report.27.09.2026.pdf` each list the date, unticked;
    - a numeric date's *own* separator (the one its three groups share, compared by class: `/`, `.`, or any hyphen as above) right before it with a digit before that (`1/05/12/1980`, `00-2026-09-28`), or right after it with a digit after that (`2026-09-27-01`, `05.12.1980.17`): a longer number of the same shape;
    - two or more digits glued to a date's end, a digit in another script, or one digit after a two digit year (`27 September 202612`, `27 September 2026١`, `05.12.801`): a longer number. Exactly one ASCII digit after a four digit year is a footnote marker (third update of 2026-10-11, below).

    The second and third rejections (the ones about what stands *before* a numeric date) have two exemptions, each lifting both, and the birth word's exemption is shared by a date label:
    - **The joiner.** A *joiner* is exactly one hyphen as above or one `/` (never `.`). At a date's end, the own separator rejection is lifted when a full date starts right after the joiner, read with this detector's own numeric and written forms (day ranges and `of` included) with no start or end check of that second date, so each check costs a constant. At a date's start, where the code point before the date is a joiner, both rejections are lifted when the stretch from the end of the last date the scan listed up to that joiner is empty or *only a time*: at most `TIME_LONGEST` (40) code points, holding at least one ASCII digit, every code point one of a digit, whitespace, `:`, `.`, `,`, `+`, `@`, a hyphen as above, or a letter (`\p{L}`), with no run of more than `TIME_LETTERS_MOST` (5) letters in a row. The last listed date's end is kept only for a date actually listed and is never reset; with none listed, nothing is lifted. So in `01/05/1980-31/05/1980` and `2026-09-01-2026-09-30` the first date's end sees a full date beyond the hyphen, and the second date's start sees an empty stretch; in `27/09/2026 10:00-28/09/2026`, `27-09-2026 10:00-12-10-2026`, `2026-09-27 10:00-2026-09-28`, `2026-09-27T10:00:00+0100-2026-09-28`, `2026-09-27T10:00:00EST-2026-09-28`, `2026-09-27 @ 10:00-2026-09-28`, `2026-09-27 10h00-2026-09-28`, `2026-09-27 10:00 PM EST-2026-09-28` and `2026-09-27 10:00 UTC+1-2026-09-28` the stretch is a time; each date is its own row. In `2026-09-27-01` nothing beyond the hyphen is a date and no date was listed before, so it stays one code. In `05.12.1980.10.12.1980` neither date is found, because a dot never joins. In `2026-09-27 10:00 Monday-2026-09-28` and `2026-09-27 ref INV-2026-09-28` the first date only is found: `Monday` is six letters, and ` ref INV` holds no digit. The stretch check reads at most `TIME_LONGEST` code points and runs only at a start whose own separator or letter rule would otherwise drop the date, so it stays linear (INV-7).
    - **The birth word, and the date label.** When a word in `BIRTH_WORDS`, in any case and with no letter or digit right before it, ends exactly at the date's start, or exactly before the one separator at `at - 1`, the date is listed (`DOB27/09/1980`, `DOB-12/05/1980`, `D.O.B.12.05.1980`, `Born-05.12.1980`). The same test lifts the glued letter rejection for a written date (`DOB27 September 1980`). The date is ticked by AC-10's `wordBefore` alone, whose window ends at the date's start and so sees the glued word as whole (third update of 2026-10-11: the separate tick call the second update asked for was dead, and goes). A word in `DATE_LABELS` (`date`, `dated`, `dt`, `exp`, `expiry`, `expires`, `expired`, `issued`, `valid`, `from`, `to`, `until`, `on`, `effective`, `signed`), tested the same way through its own list, lifts the same rejections and leaves the date unticked (`Date27/09/2026`, `Dated27 September 2026`, `Exp.31.12.2026`, `Date-27/09/2026`, `Valid-31/12/2026`, `from27/09/2026 to28/09/2026`). Each window read is the list's longest word plus one code point, so the checks cost a constant. `Reborn27/09/1980`, `Toronto27/09/2026` and `photo-27/09/2026` give none.
    - **The footnote marker** (third update of 2026-10-11). Exactly one ASCII digit right after a date, with no digit of any script after it, is a marker and lies outside the row, when the date's year has four digits. A numeric date's last group is read as up to `MAX_GROUP_DIGITS + 1` digits; when the whole is no date under `isNumericDate` and the group holds three or more digits, the text without the group's last digit is tried, and it is the date when `isNumericDate` accepts it and its year is four digits (`27/09/20261` reads `27/09/2026`, `2026-09-271` reads `2026-09-27`, `05.12.19800` reads `05.12.1980`; `05.12.801` and `2026-09-2712` read nothing). A written date's year may be followed by one such digit (`27 September 20261`, `Sep 27 2026²`). A numeric date's own separator with a digit after it (the third rejection, at the date's end) is also lifted when the separator is a dot, the year has four digits, and exactly one ASCII digit follows the dot (`geboren am 12.05.1980.¹`, a sentence's full stop and a marker); after a hyphen or a slash it is not (`2026-09-27-1`, `27/09/2026/1`). `27/09/2026¹-28/09/2026` lists both dates: the first ends before its marker, and the joiner's stretch (`1`) is a time by the stretch rule.

    Everything else after a date leaves it listed: a letter glued to it (`05.12.1980a`, `2026-09-27Tuesday`, `2026-09-27T10:00:00Z`, `27 September 2026AD`), a separator of any kind followed by a letter (`12/05/1980-Smith`, `2026-09-27-A1`), a different separator followed by digits (`05.12.1980-17`, `2026-09-27/01`), a sentence's full stop, a time, a footnote marker. The row is always the date alone; the time and the marker stay in the file. A separator of a different class right before a numeric date never cuts it either, whatever is before the separator, unless that is a letter before a hyphen or a slash: `5-7/6/2026` lists `7/6/2026`, `10:00-28/09/2026` lists `28/09/2026`, `Fig 3/4/05a` lists `3/4/05`, and `2026-09-27 10:00 EST.2026-09-28` lists both dates.
- **`card`** (release 3; its finder rebuilt by the update of 2026-10-08, AC-28). In order:
  1. **Units.** A *group* is one or more ASCII digits. A *unit* is one group, or several each joined to the one before by a single *gluing* hyphen (`-`, U+2010 to U+2015, or U+2212 minus, with a digit right on each side). A hyphen glues unless the group after it is followed right away by a slash and a digit, or the group before it has a digit and a slash right before it (`4111111111111111-12/28`, `4111 1111 1111 1111–12/28`, `12/28-4111111111111111`); such a hyphen *parts* the groups and belongs to neither. A unit begins where neither a digit nor a gluing hyphen comes right before it, and runs on while a digit, or a gluing hyphen and a digit, follows. A unit is never cut. A *bare* unit holds no hyphen. A bare unit of one or two digits followed right away by a slash and a digit (the `12` of `12/28`, the `05` of `05/12/2026`) is not a card unit: a run ends before it, and nothing here reads it.
  2. **Runs.** A *run* is one unit, then any number of further units, each after exactly one space (U+0020, which is also what a line join leaves). Anything else ends the run: a second space, a tab, a letter, a dot, a slash, a parting hyphen, any other punctuation, the block's end.
  3. **Windows.** A *window* is consecutive units of one run: either one unit alone, bare or hyphenated, or two or more bare units whose first holds 4 to 6 digits, whose last holds 1 to 6, and every other 3 to 6 (`CARD_GROUP_DIGITS`). A window holds 13 to 19 digits, counted as it grows; it stops growing once its count passes 19 or the next unit breaks the sizes, so a unit longer than 19 digits is read once and never judged. So a hyphenated card is always a window on its own, an unbroken card never shares a window with a neighbour, and a group of one or two digits can only close a window. A *tolerated* window (third update of 2026-10-11) is a window read the same way but allowed up to 20 digits, whose last unit ends in a group of two or more digits (so the marker is counted in that unit's 1 to 6 limit and in the window's count), and whose digits pass (step 4) once that unit's last digit is left out: a card with a footnote marker glued to its end (`4111 1111 1111 11111`, `41111111111111111`, `4111-1111-1111-11111`). The digit left out is the unit's last as counted, never one of the 19 a longer unit stores, so a 20 digit unit is judged on the 19 it holds. A unit whose last group is one digit is never tolerated (`4111-1111-1111-1111-1` gives no card, since a row ending in a hyphen is no card), and a unit of 21 or more digits is never tolerated, so two glued digits still give no card.
  4. **Passing.** A window passes when its digits, read as one number, pass Luhn and match a prefix and length in `CARD_BRANDS`: Visa `4` (13, 16, 19), Mastercard `51` to `55` and `2221` to `2720` (16), American Express `34`, `37` (15), Discover `6011`, `644` to `649`, `65` (16 to 19), JCB `3528` to `3589` (16 to 19), Diners Club `300` to `305`, `36`, `38`, `39` (14 to 19), UnionPay `62` (16 to 19).
  5. **Choosing** (rewritten 2026-10-10). Over each run from left to right, at each unit not yet taken, take the longest window from there that passes; the card's row runs to that window's last unit. Then, for each later unit up to the row's last unit, take the longest window from that unit that passes, and when it ends past the row's last unit, carry the row to its end; the units this adds are read the same way in turn. Scanning resumes at the unit after the row's last unit; a unit where no window passes is passed over. Windows from one start are nested, so longest means most digits and most units alike. So every window of the run that passes and overlaps a card lies inside that card's row. Nothing before a window's first unit is checked beyond step 1, so `12/4111111111111111` and `1.4111111111111111` are found, as before. Tolerated windows (third update of 2026-10-11) join the choosing: at a unit where no window passes, the longest tolerated window from there is the card's row, ending before the dropped digit; and when the longest tolerated window from the row's own start or from a later unit inside the row ends past the row's last unit, it carries the row to its last unit the way a passing window does, the row then ending before the dropped digit. The row's end is compared as the pair (last unit, whether a digit is dropped): a window that reaches a later unit wins, and at the same last unit a passing window beats a tolerated one in whatever order the units are visited, so the row then ends at the unit's end. So `4111 1111 1111 11111` lists `4111 1111 1111 1111`; `2226 4111 1111 1111 11111`, where the chance window `2226 4111 1111 1111` passes, lists `2226 4111 1111 1111 1111` (every card digit in one row, with the marker outside); and `4111 1111 1111 1111 0031` lists `4111 1111 1111 1111 003`, since the window with the CVV passes once its marker is left out. So `2226 4111 1111 1111 1111`, where `2226 4111 1111 1111` passes by chance and `4111 1111 1111 1111` is the card, lists one row of all twenty digits; a 19 digit card whose first 16 digits also pass is taken whole alone and before ` 12/28`, `-12/28`, ` 12 28`, `.5` or `/5`; `4111 1111 1111 1111 003 1234` takes the CVV `003`, because `4111 1111 1111 1111 003` passes too; and `4111 1111 1111 1111 020 7946 0958` lists the card and the phone number each whole, because no window holding `020` passes. The clear end rule of 2026-10-08 is gone: a window reaching the run's end is no longer preferred, since the longest passing window from each start is always taken (the last that passes of the nested windows from a start, where the shortest was the first). Steps 1 to 4, `CARD_GROUP_DIGITS` and the short slash group that ends a run are unchanged.
  6. **Span.** A card's span runs from the first digit of its first unit to just after the last digit of its last unit, in code points, holding the spaces and gluing hyphens between its groups and nothing after its last digit.
  7. **Tick.** A card starts ticked (AC-10) when any window inside its row passes whole, from its start or from a later unit, whether it is a whole run or part of one; a row in which only tolerated windows were found starts unticked (third update of 2026-10-11). So `4111 1111 1111 11111` is unticked, and `4521 4111 1111 1111 1111` is ticked whether or not its twenty digits less one pass by chance.

  Linear: one pass builds the units and runs, and from each unit at most seven windows are read (the first unit holds 4 or more digits, every later one at least 1 and all but the last at least 3, and growth stops past 20 digits with the tolerance); each window is judged at most twice per start (as read, and without its last digit), a constant per unit. Each unit is a start at most once, either where scanning stands or while a row is carried, because scanning resumes after the row.
- **`iban`** (release 3). Two capital letters naming a country in `IBAN_LENGTHS`, two check digits, then capital letters and digits, unbroken or in groups of four split by single spaces (the last group may be shorter), with the country's exact length once spaces are dropped. Moving the first four characters to the end, turning each letter into two digits (A is 10) and taking the remainder by 97, digit by digit, must give 1. `IBAN_LENGTHS` is written from the SWIFT IBAN Registry, and a test pins a sample (GB 22, DE 22, FR 27, NL 18, ES 24, IT 27, IE 22). No letter or digit comes right before it. A letter or digit may come right after it, since the country's length ends it (amended 2026-10-10, AC-21): the characters past that length are never read, the grouped form still ends exactly at that length (inside a group of four if it must), and the IBAN is still ticked.
- **`us-ssn`** (release 3). Three, two and four digits split by one hyphen or one space, the same both times. The area is never `000`, `666` or `900` to `999`, the group never `00`, the serial never `0000`. A bare run of nine digits under the same rules counts only when `SSN`, `SS#`, `SS No` or `Social Security` (any case, a whole word) ends within `KEYWORD_REACH` characters before it, in its block. No digit touches its start. At its end, a separated number may be followed by exactly one ASCII digit with no digit after it (third update of 2026-10-11): a footnote marker, outside the row, and the number starts unticked (`123-45-6789¹`, `123 45 6789¹`, `123-45-67890`); two digits give none, and a bare run with any digit after it gives none.
- **`uk-nino`** (release 3). Two letters, six digits, then an optional suffix `A` to `D`, in any case, with optional single spaces between the letters and digits, between the digit pairs, and before the suffix (`QQ 12 34 56 C`, `qq123456c`, `QQ123456`). The first letter is never D, F, I, Q, U or V; the second never D, F, I, O, Q, U or V; the pair never BG, GB, KN, NK, NT, TN or ZZ. No letter or digit comes right before it. After it, as before: a suffix (spaced or not) with no letter or digit after it joins the match, and otherwise the match ends after the six digits when no letter or digit touches them. Amended 2026-10-10 (AC-23, AC-10): when a letter (any `\p{L}`, as `ALPHANUMERIC` reads it) touches the six digits, the number is still found but unticked, and that letter joins its row when it is `A` to `D` in any case, whatever follows it (`AB123456CD` and `AB123456C7` list `AB123456C`, `AB123456Date` lists `AB123456D`, `AB123456Ename` lists `AB123456`, `AB 12 34 56Cname` lists `AB 12 34 56C`). A digit after that letter does not matter, because the letter touched the six digits first: `AB123456C7` lists `AB123456C`, unticked. When a digit touches the six digits themselves, nothing is found (`AB1234567`, `AB1234561`). A suffix after a space is read as before, so `AB 12 34 56 Cname` lists `AB 12 34 56`, ticked.

Constants in `src/detect`, each commented as a rule about a pattern, not a cap on the visitor, like the engine's geometry constants: `PHONE_REGIONS`, `PHONE_READINGS`, `MAX_WINDOW_DIGITS` (18), `MAX_PARSES_PER_GROUP` (10, derived from `PHONE_READINGS` and exported so the adversarial test asserts the same number), `EXTENSION_MARKERS`, `PRECEDENCE`, `KEYWORD_REACH` (32 characters), the phone, birth, date label (`DATE_LABELS`, third update of 2026-10-11) and SSN word lists, `CARD_BRANDS`, `CARD_GROUP_DIGITS` (a spaced card's first unit 4 to 6 digits, its last 1 to 6, every other 3 to 6; exported beside `CARD_BRANDS` so a test pins it), `IBAN_LENGTHS`, and the NI prefix rules.

**Key invariants**:

- **INV-1**: Detection reads only the prepared review copy, through `EXTRACTION_OPTIONS`, never `CHECK_EXTRACTION_OPTIONS`, so the visitor reviews what the page shows (spec 0004, INV-9).
- **INV-2**: A match has a target exactly when it is not blocked. A blocked match cannot be ticked (the reducer) and cannot be redacted (no target, so `unsupported`).
- **INV-3**: Detection and `validateTargets` answer through the same predicates. An unblocked match passes target validation alone and with any other unblocked matches, because each check is decided per target.
- **INV-4**: Quads, offsets and page geometry never cross the boundary (spec 0002, INV-2). `FoundMatch` is an engine type, and the worker strips it to a `ReviewMatch`.
- **INV-5**: Document text inside the find step and `src/detect` is never logged, stored or sent. `src/detect` imports nothing that touches the page, the network or storage, and has no console.
- **INV-6**: `before` and `after` are at most `contextChars` code points each (spec 0002, INV-9).
- **INV-7**: Every detector is pure and linear time (AC-16), and the phone detector parses at most `MAX_PARSES_PER_GROUP` windows per digit group.
- **INV-8**: `libphonenumber-js` is imported only by `src/detect`, and `@/detect` only by `src/engine` (AC-17).
- **INV-9**: Every `DetectorKind` has a detector, a place in `PRECEDENCE`, a label, an icon, a noun and a tick rule, as typed records, so a new kind cannot ship half done (AC-24).
- **INV-10**: Detection fails closed: a readable page it cannot read fails the open (AC-12), and a check that cannot finish blocks rather than passes (every predicate is written so `NaN` or a throw counts against the match).
- **INV-11**: Detection finds matches by walking characters, never through `search()`, so no hit cap can drop one (AC-25). Lint holds it (below).
- **INV-12**: One character reader serves detection, validation, the record and the self check, and it returns whole code points (AC-26). Nothing downstream of it indexes text by UTF-16 unit.
- **INV-13**: A phone match is whole units, and no unit belongs to two matches, so no number is found with a digit of its neighbour, and none is lost because a neighbour made its run too long (AC-27).
- **INV-14**: A full numeric date is never read as a phone number, and the phone detector asks the same `isNumericDate` the `date` detector will. So in release 3 every numeric date is left for `date`, whatever `PRECEDENCE` says (AC-2, AC-19).
- **INV-15**: A card match is whole units of one run, except that a row may end one digit before its last unit's end, where that digit is a footnote marker (a tolerated window, *Detectors* `card` step 3, third update of 2026-10-11). An unbroken run of digits, or digits glued by hyphens (a hyphen next to an expiry or a date parts instead, step 1), is never otherwise cut into a card, and no unit belongs to two cards (AC-28). Every window of a run that passes, or is tolerated, lies inside one card's row, so no digit of such a window but its marker is left without a row (amended 2026-10-10 and 2026-10-11).
- **INV-16**: Resolving overlaps never leaves a character a detector found without a match. A higher kind's span is kept whole; a lower kind's span keeps every stretch no higher match holds (AC-3).
- **INV-17**: A boundary rule drops a value only where it would be read out of a longer code (added 2026-10-10, reworded by the second and third updates). A letter or digit right before a date, an IBAN or a National Insurance number still drops it, unless the thing glued before a date is a birth word or a date label (AC-10, AC-19). A full, real date in a form the detector reads is never dropped for what follows it or what it is joined to (AC-19): it is dropped only (1) for a letter glued to its start, (2) for a letter before a hyphen or a slash before it, never a dot, (3) for its own separator running on with digits on either side, or (4) for two or more digits glued to its end, a digit in another script, or one digit after a two digit year; and (2) and (3) are lifted when a birth word or a date label is what is glued, when the separator is a joiner with only a time between it and the last date listed, when, at a date's end, the separator is a joiner with a full date right after it, or when, at a date's end, the separator is a dot after a four digit year followed by exactly one ASCII digit with no digit of any script after it. Exactly one ASCII digit stuck after a date with a four digit year, after a separated Social Security number, or after a card's last unit is a footnote marker: the value is listed with the digit outside its row, never dropped (AC-19, AC-20, AC-22). An IBAN is never dropped for what follows it; a National Insurance number is dropped only for a digit, and a letter makes it unticked instead (AC-21, AC-23). A missed date in a form the detector reads means a rejection is too wide, never that a boundary form is missing; a date in a form it does not read (`05-Jan-2026`, `2026.09.27`, `2026/9/7`, under *Consequences*) is a form to add, not a boundary to widen.

**Security model**: an anonymous visitor, one tab, no account and no server call; detection is the same for the free and paid tiers. Everything detection finds is personal data (email, phone, dates of birth, card, bank, SSN and NI numbers, which GDPR, PCI DSS and US state privacy law all care about), and none of it leaves the device, so RedactNest processes none of it. Match text and context live on the main thread for the life of the session only (spec 0002, INV-1 and INV-3). Every document string is rendered as React text, which escapes it (spec 0003, INV-7). A crafted document can make detection slow only in the visitor's own tab, and AC-16 bounds that.

**Configuration required**: no new environment variables. `NEXT_PUBLIC_MATCH_CONTEXT_CHARS` (spec 0002) sets the context window. `libphonenumber-js` joins `dependencies`.

## Consequences

**Positive**:
- Release 1 is a usable tool the day slice 1 lands, and every later kind is a detector, a fixture and a label.
- A row that can be ticked is a row the engine will accept, because both ask the same predicates. The session loss spec 0004 warned about is gone for slant, images and visible replacement text.
- `src/detect` is pure and has no MuPDF, so its tests are fast string tests, and the engine grows by one step, not a second job.
- A visitor is told both what was found and what was not looked for.
- Phone numbers side by side are each found: a staff table's column, a call log line, a comma list. None loses a digit to its neighbour, and no neighbour hides one.
- ZIP+4, Social Security shapes, bare digit IDs and numeric dates no longer crowd the phone group, and in release 3 a date of birth written `05.12.1980` reaches the `date` group, where a birth word ticks it.
- The phone detector's worst measured time on 100,000 adversarial characters falls from about 2.1 s to about 0.5 s (prototype), and its cost is proved by a parse count that does not depend on the machine.
- A card written beside its expiry, a CVV, a date, another card or a row number, typed against its expiry with a hyphen or dash, or spaced the way payment forms print it, is found and removed by default (update of 2026-10-08). Before, every one of those layouts left the card in the file with no row, and a spaced American Express or Diners Club card beside its expiry was listed as a phone number cut from its first ten digits.
- No character a detector found is left without a row by the overlap rule: where a higher match cuts into a lower one, the rest of the lower one is its own row (AC-3, INV-16).
- No digit of a real card is left without a row in any measured layout (update of 2026-10-10): a number before a spaced card, and more digits after a 17 to 19 digit card, used to leave one of its groups in the file beside a ticked row that looked complete. A CVV with more digits after it now goes with the card when the two pass together, as it already did when it ended the run.
- Date ranges are found (update of 2026-10-10): two full dates joined by a hyphen, a dash or a slash, as employment histories, leave records and contracts write them, day ranges such as `3–5 June 2026`, the full date of a range across months such as `28 May-3 June 2026`, and both dates of an ISO interval, all of which gave no row. So is a date of birth in a system export's `1980-05-12T00:00:00Z`.
- A real date is never dropped for what follows it (second update of 2026-10-10): both dates of a booking, leave or event interval whatever form its times take (`27/09/2026 10:00-28/09/2026 11:00`, offsets, zone names, `@`, `10h00`, `PM EST`), a date of birth followed by a hyphen and a name, glued to a word, or glued to its own label (`DOB27/09/1980`, `D.O.B.12.05.1980`, each now ticked), a spaced range such as `June 5 - 7, 2026`, and the date after a day range such as `5-7/6/2026`, each of which gave no row or lost its second date. The rule is general, so the next way a document joins a date to a time is listed without a change here; only the two measured rejections can drop a date in a form the detector reads, and INV-17 says such a miss means one of them is too wide.
- An IBAN glued to the next word or a footnote marker is found and removed by default, and a National Insurance number glued to a word is listed for the visitor to judge (update of 2026-10-10).
- A footnote marker after a date, a Social Security number or a card no longer hides it (third update of 2026-10-11): a date of birth with a reference mark set flush after it is listed and ticked, a Social Security number or a card with one is listed for the visitor to judge, and a label run into its date by extraction (`Exp.31.12.2026`, `Date-27/09/2026`, `Issued27/09/2026`) or an abbreviation before a dotted date (`w.e.f.27.09.2026`) lists the date. Before, each gave no row and no warning.

**Negative / tradeoffs**:
- The footnote tolerance lists some codes that end in one digit too many (third update of 2026-10-11), each an unticked row: measured on random codes, `N/N/NNNNN` lists a date 1.9% of the time and `20NN-NN-NNN` 3.2%, the other glued digit shapes 0.0% to 0.2%; a 17 digit number lists an unticked card 2.5% of the time (on top of the 0.7% a 17 digit brand already lists ticked), a 20 digit number 1.6%, a licence key of five four digit groups 1.8%, and a spaced `4 4 4 5` number 2.8% more than before; a number written as 3, 2 and 5 digits lists an unticked Social Security number 89% of the time, the SSA's pass rate on random digits, since nothing but a footnoted number is written that way. A card's row can take a group after the card and leave its marker (`4111 1111 1111 1111 0031` takes `003`), 6% more often than before on a CVV with a marker after it. A date of birth followed by two markers, which is also what the tenth footnote and up look like (`Born 12/05/1980¹⁰`, read as `198010`), or a marker in another script, is still dropped (`Born 12/05/1980¹²`, `Born 12/05/1980١`), as is one after a two digit year (`05.12.80¹`, `12/05/80¹`, under Follow-up) and one after a hyphen or a slash of the date's own class (`2026-09-27-1`); a Social Security number written bare with a marker (`SSN 123456789¹`) and a National Insurance number with no suffix and a marker (`AB123456¹`), each under Follow-up, are too.
- Through `detect`, a Social Security number with a marker can leave a ticked phone piece (third update of 2026-10-11): `SSN 223-45-6789¹` reads as a possible US number to the phone detector, the Social Security row wins by `PRECEDENCE`, and the marker is left as a phone piece `1` with the phone row's tick (AC-3, INV-16). Nothing is lost; one character is removed by default that need not be, under the wrong label. The phone Follow-up holds it with the other cases of a phone window reaching into a neighbour.
- Dotted file names and abbreviations before a dotted date list an unticked row (third update): `report.27.09.2026.pdf` and `No.27.09.2026` each list the date 100% of the time on random samples (0% before), as `report_2026-09-27.pdf` already did; `report-27-09-2026-v2.pdf` and `v1.05.12.1980` still do not. A label glued to a date is listed only when the label is in `DATE_LABELS` and has no letter or digit before it: `Updated27/09/2026`, `Rev-27/09/2026` and any other word glued directly or by a hyphen or a slash stay a recorded miss, and so does the second date of `27/09/2026to28/09/2026`, where `to` has a digit before it; the list is a list, so a label found missing is a word to add, each measured against two and three letter codes before it is.
- The time stretch before a joiner judges characters, not meaning, so a date shortly before a dated code can lift the code's own separator rule (second update of 2026-10-10, recorded by the third): `Paid 27/09/2026 ref 1/05/12/1980` lists `05/12/1980` out of the code, and `Leave 2026-09-01-2026-09-30-01` lists `09-30-01` out of the code's tail while `2026-09-30`, part of the code by the own separator rule, is dropped. Extra unticked rows and a code that was never a date; no real date is lost.
- Rule based detection misses whatever does not look like its pattern: a phone number written in words, a national number from a region outside `PHONE_REGIONS`, a date with a non English month, and names and addresses entirely. The coverage note says so; nothing else can.
- Some runs can still fail after a clean review, for causes spec 0004 records and no check before removal can see: a size or scaling change inside a text object next to a match, a combining mark drawn at zero width, a file that shows lines with the `'` or `"` operators (which fails every run on that file), a text watermark crossing a match, and replacement text wider than a match whose string equals its glyphs (MuPDF.js 1.28.1 reports no replacement spans, and both extraction modes read such a match exactly as they read one that redacts cleanly, measured). Each ends in `failed`, never a leak.
- One row per occurrence makes a long list on a document with a repeated footer; feature 8's select all per group is the relief.
- A value wrapped mid word or at a hyphen is missed, and an email wrapped at a dot in its local part lists only the part after the break.
- The worker's chunk grows by libphonenumber-js and its `max` metadata, 157 KB of metadata before compression (measured on 1.13.14), fetched once and cached.
- The phone group carries more unticked rows: plausible numbers that are not valid, and valid numbers written as bare digits. Nothing is hidden and nothing unsure is removed by default, at the cost of a longer list to scan.
- The phone candidate grammar is ours now, not the library's matcher: units, gaps, brackets, the slash, extension markers and the readings table are code we maintain. A format the library's matcher found and our grammar does not is a miss; slice 5's rule that every case in today's phone tests still passes, plus the "every grouping kept" cases, is the guard, and a new real world format means a new test.
- The trunk rule drops a UK number written with neither its `0` nor a country code. With 10 digits starting 2 to 9 it is still read as a US number, so its digits are listed either way, but one starting `1` (`161 496 0000`) is not found.
- A phone number written in decimal digits other than ASCII `0` to `9` is found only when it is written with a `+`. Every row of `PHONE_READINGS` but `+` tests its prefix as ASCII digits, so `٠٢٠ ٧٩٤٦ ٠٩٥٨` and `(٢١٢) ٥٥٥-٠١٢٣` (Arabic Indic digits, U+0660 to U+0669) join a run but get no reading and are never parsed; the same holds for Eastern Arabic Indic digits (U+06F0 to U+06F9, used for Persian and Urdu). A window starting with `+` is parsed, and libphonenumber-js reads both of those digit sets, so `+٤٤ ٢٠ ٧٩٤٦ ٠٩٥٨` is found and ticked. It does not read Devanagari digits, so `+४४ २० ७९४६ ०९५८` is not found either way (all measured through `detect` on 2026-09-28). Fullwidth digits are unaffected, because NFKC turns them into ASCII before detection sees them.
- The phone budget is 2 seconds, twice every other detector's. A crafted page can hold one detection read for up to that long per 100,000 characters, and a cancel is noticed only when that read ends (AC-11).
- A run of numbers is split only at spaces, brackets and slashes, so two numbers joined by a hyphen or dot with nothing else between them (`020-7946-0958-020-7946-0321`) are not found, and a number with four or more spaces inside it is read as two pieces. The shorthand after a slash (`0959` in `020 7946 0958/0959`) stays in the file.
- Valid first choosing can take a stray digit beside a number when together they make a valid number (`Ste 1 212 555 0123` lists `1 212 555 0123`), so one extra character is removed.
- Junk that is possible under a reading, such as `22 22 22 22 22`, is still listed unticked, because every grouping is kept.
- The one character extension markers can take a short token that is not an extension (`020 7946 0958 x2 monitors` removes `x2` as well). That removes a few characters too many; the alternative, dropping `x` and `#`, would leave real extensions (a person's desk line) in the file.
- A page holding a code point above U+FFFF costs one `asJSON()` per read on top of the walk, and a line where MuPDF's two views of the text disagree refuses the open or the run rather than guessing.
- Every page is read three more times at open (two structured texts and one image walk), so opening costs more on a 50 page document; measured in Follow-up, not guessed.
- On text whose extraction order runs against its visual order, quads are proved by redacting cleanly (AC-6), not against `page.search()`, and the fixture only simulates it, because the repository has no right to left font.
- Reading cards out of a longer run lists false cards in spaced numbers that are not cards, ticked: measured on random digits, 3.6% of 18 digit numbers grouped 4 4 4 4 2 (0.8% before), 5.4% at 20 digits in fours, 6.3% at 22 and 8.1% at 24 (none before); 16 digits in fours stay at 2.8%. Each shows with its context and can be unticked; a tracking or reference number written that way is the likely source.
- A card's row can hold a neighbour's digits (update of 2026-10-10, replacing two recorded limits on cards that lost a group). Where a window that passes by chance overlaps a real card, the row covers both. Measured on random valid cards: a 4 to 6 digit number, or two 4 digit groups, before a spaced card joins its row 1.6% to 4.4% of the time (it did before, as the false window, but then took the card's first groups and left the rest); and a group after the card joins its row 0.6% to 8.3% of the time (an expiry `03 28` 0.6%, a dotted date's day 0.8%, a reference 3.9%, `1228` 5.9%, a CVV 6.7%, a UK phone number's area code on the next line 7.3%, a Social Security number's first group 8.3%). The rest of such a number keeps its own row, ticked as it was (AC-3), so nothing is left without a row; the cost is a few digits removed with the card, and a card row whose text shows them. False cards in spaced numbers that are not cards are no more common, and their rows grow by under half a digit on average (16.8 to 17.2 digits at 48 digits in fours, 16.0 to 16.1 at 24).
- A National Insurance number, an IBAN or a date with a letter or digit right before it, and a numeric date with a letter before a hyphen or a slash before it (`REF-05.12.1980`, `Smith-12/05/1980`, `INV/12/05/2026`), is still not found (update of 2026-10-10, kept by the second and third updates, which exempt only a glued birth word or date label and a letter before a dot): that is how a value reads out of the end of a longer code, so the start rule stays for every kind, and measured on random codes it is what keeps every `REF-` code and every dated file name (`report-27-09-2026-v2.pdf`) out of the Dates group. How often a PDF's text puts a word right against the start of a value was not measured; the probe of 2026-10-08 showed that MuPDF reads glyphs as touching only when they are drawn 1 point or less apart at 12 point.
- A numeric date whose own separator runs on with digits is still not found (second update of 2026-10-10): `2026-09-27-01`, `1/05/12/1980` and `05.12.1980.17` give no date row (the first two are listed as phone numbers, as before, `2026-09-27-01` ticked as a valid US number written with hyphens). Measured on random codes, dropping this rule would list 62% of version numbers (`1.2.3.4`), 35% of French phone numbers (`01-02-03-04-05`), 9% of sort codes with a fourth group and 15% to 27% of serials of two digit groups as dates. The cost is a hyphen dated or ISO date after a hyphen where what stands between it and the last date is not a time: a word of six letters or more, or no digit at all (`2026-09-27 10:00 Monday-2026-09-28` and `2026-09-27 ref INV-2026-09-28` list the first date only), and the first date of a dotted pair (`05.12.1980.10.12.1980` lists neither, since a dot never joins).
- Dated codes with any other shape now list an unticked row (second update of 2026-10-10), the measured noise of the general rule: a date followed by a different separator and digits (`2026-09-27/01`, `05.12.1980-17`), by a separator and letters (`2026-09-27-A1`, `2026-09-27-XX-01`), or glued to a letter (`2026-09-27a`, `Fig 3/4/05a`), the date inside a file name with no letter before its separator (`report_2026-09-27.pdf`) and a URL path with nothing before its first slash (`/2026/09/27/slug`; with a host, `example.com/2026/09/27/slug`, the letter before the slash still drops it) each list the date, every one of those shapes 100% of the time on random samples (0% before). Each shows with its context and costs a glance; none is removed by default, because no birth word stands before it. Of 1,008 detection test strings and fixture lines, eight results change, every one a real date now listed. The noise was measured on random shapes and on the repository's own test strings and fixture lines; no natural text corpus was run, so a document type that writes many dated codes of these shapes will show more unticked rows than measured.
- Date forms the detector has no pattern for are still missed whatever stands beside them (noted by the second update's cross check and widened by the third review, not new): a written month with separators (`05-Jan-2026`, `27/Sep/2026`, `27-September-2026`), a year first date with dots (`2026.09.27`), an unseparated date (`20260927`), a written date with a two digit year (`27 Sep 26`), a comma with no space (`15 March,2026`), a year first date with a one digit month or day (`2026/9/7`, `2026-9-7`, as spreadsheet and East Asian exports write them), spaced separators (`27 / 09 / 2026`, `27 . 09 . 2026`), the legal formula (`27th day of September 2026`, `Dated this 1st day of May, 2026`), a German style ordinal (`27. September 2026`), and a comma or a full stop set oddly (`September, 27 2026`, `Sept.27, 2026`). These are forms to add, under the Follow-up below, not boundaries to widen.
- An IBAN glued at its end can be read out of a longer code (update of 2026-10-10): measured, 1.0% of random codes that open with a country code and two digits and run on past that country's length list an IBAN, ticked, the 1 in 97 chance that the check passes. Codes of that shape are rare outside banking.
- A National Insurance number glued to a letter starts unticked, so a real one glued to a word stays in the file unless the visitor ticks it; and product codes of two letters, six digits and more letters now list unticked rows in the National Insurance group (measured: 55% of random codes of that shape, after HMRC's prefix rules). With a suffix, a digit after it does not matter: `AB123456C7`, and a footnote marker after a suffix (`AB123456C¹`), list `AB123456C`, unticked.
- An accepted miss (update of 2026-10-10): a National Insurance number with a digit right after its six digits gives no row. That covers a real number written with no suffix and followed by a footnote marker (`AB123456¹`, which NFKC reads as `AB1234561`), or glued to a word that starts with a digit (`AB1234567`). A digit there is far more often part of a longer number. Allowing it would list every tracking number shaped `AB123456789GB` (55% of random ones, after HMRC's prefix rules), and would read `on 05 12 1980` as the National Insurance number `on 05 12 19` (measured on the scratch copy, *Evidence* in `rationale.md`).
- A day range row holds both days, so `Pages 3-5 June 2026` lists `3-5 June 2026` (unticked, like every date with no birth word), and month first `Pages June 3 - 5, 2026` lists `June 3 - 5, 2026` since the second update; day first, a range written with spaces (`3 - 5 June 2026`) still lists only `5 June 2026`, leaving `3 -`, on purpose, so a numbered heading (`Table 2 - 14 March 2026`) keeps its number. A range across months lists only its full date: `28 May-3 June 2026` leaves `28 May` in the file, a day and month with no year, which `date` never reads alone; and a day range before a numeric date leaves its first day (`5-7/6/2026` leaves `5-`).
- A written date beside a separator, or with a letter glued after its year, is listed whatever is beyond it, so a written date inside a code (`REF-27 September 2026`, `27 September 2026AD`) lists an unticked row. One test string changed under this rule (`27 September 2026AD`); written dates inside codes are rare.
- The second date of an ISO interval with plain clock times is listed as a phone number, not a date (second update of 2026-10-10): through `detect`, `2026-09-27 10:00-2026-09-28 11:00` gives `2026-09-27` and the unticked phone row `00-2026-09-28 11`, because the phone detector reads `00-2026-09-28` as one hyphen glued unit, which is not a date as a whole, and `PRECEDENCE` puts `phone` above `date`. The date detector lists `2026-09-28` on its own; the overlap step drops it inside the phone row. Nothing is lost, the visitor can tick the phone row, but the label and the row text are wrong. The phone Follow-up holds it with the other cases of a phone window reaching into a neighbour.
- Two cards side by side on one run can share one row (update of 2026-10-10): measured, 6.8% of pairs of random valid cards, where a window across the two passes by chance, and always for a card repeated end to end such as `4242 4242 4242 4242` twice. Both are removed by default either way; the visitor cannot keep one while removing the other.
- A spaced card in groups no issuer or payment form prints (pairs, a group of one or two digits anywhere but last, a first group of fewer than four or more than six digits) is no longer found, though it was before; written unbroken or hyphenated it still is.
- A card glued to other digits with no space between (`41111111111111111228`) is still not found, because an unbroken run is never cut, and nor is one glued by a hyphen with no slash after it (`4111111111111111-1228`), because that hyphen glues as it does in a licence key. MuPDF's ordinary read puts a space where a gap of about a sixth of an em is drawn (measured at 12 point: a gap of 2 points or more reads as a space, 1 point or less as touching digits), so only digits drawn practically touching reach detection glued.
- An overlap now leaves pieces: part of a value can show as its own row (`45 6789` as a Social Security number, `020 7946` as a phone number), and a chance reading that overlapped a real value leaves a piece too (`123 45 6789 1234` lists the Social Security number and an unticked phone piece `1234`). A piece takes the tick of the span it was cut from, so a piece of a ticked chance reading removes a few extra digits by default; never fewer.
- The card grammar is ours, like the phone grammar: units, gluing and parting hyphens, runs, group sizes and the joined row of overlapping windows are code we maintain, each pinned by a test.
