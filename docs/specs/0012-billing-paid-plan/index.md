## Summary

RedactNest gets one paid plan, Pro, at $19 a month: it lifts the page cap from the free plan's 3 pages to 50, and nothing else changes. People sign in with an emailed code through Clerk, and pay on Polar's own checkout page, where Polar acts as the merchant of record (the seller who handles tax and receipts; it trades as EdiventStudio). There is still no database: when the tool asks which plan applies, our server checks the sign in cookie itself and asks Polar whether the person holds Pro. Before any checkout, our server makes sure Polar's customer for that person is tied to their account, so a payment always lands where that check looks. The tool page still loads nothing from Clerk or Polar, and whenever it cannot confirm a plan it says so out loud instead of quietly dropping a paying user to the free cap.

## Requirements

**User stories**:
- As a free visitor whose PDF is over the cap, I want to get Pro in another tab and open the same file again here, so the paywall does not cost me the file I chose.
- As a Pro user, I want the tool to recognise my plan every time, and to tell me plainly when it cannot, so I never wonder why I am capped at 3 pages.
- As a Pro user, I want to cancel, change my card and download invoices myself, and to delete my account, without writing to anyone.
- As a visitor, I want the pricing page to tell me what Pro does today, what it costs, and that the charge shows as EdiventStudio, so nothing on my statement surprises me.
- As the operator, I want billing to need no store of my own, and a production deploy that can never run on test keys or the Polar sandbox.

  2. A 404: take the account's primary email from `currentUser()` (`primaryEmailAddress.emailAddress`), only when `primaryEmailAddress.verification.status` is `verified`. ## Decision

**Chosen option**: Option 1: Polar asked live, Clerk kept off `/tool`, and an expired sign in trusted on its signature.

Clerk holds accounts and Polar holds billing, joined only by the Clerk user id as Polar's external customer id. `/api/entitlement` reads the sign in itself from Clerk's cookie, trusting a genuine token for up to 7 days after it was issued even once Clerk's 60 second expiry has passed, and asks Polar for the Pro benefit on every check. So there is no webhook, no synced copy, no cookie of our own, no store, and no call to Clerk from the tool's one request.

**Key invariants**:
- **INV-1**: `/tool` never runs, loads or imports anything from Clerk or Polar: no `@clerk/*` or `@polar-sh/*` import reachable from it (lint), no proxy on its path or on `/api/entitlement` (matcher test), and its policy takes nothing from `OUTSIDE_SERVICES` (spec 0011, INV-2).
- **INV-2**: Fail closed, never silently. Every free answer says why (`account`), and only `signed-in` may be paid.
- **INV-3**: Every Polar customer that checkout, the billing portal or AC-25 acts on is found, tied or created on the server from the verified Clerk session (`auth()` and `currentUser()`), never from anything the browser sends: no query parameter, no body field, no header. Its external id is the session's user id; the only email used is that user's primary email, and only when Clerk reports it verified. A customer is tied only when it is live (no `deleted_at`), has no external id and its email matches ignoring case; one tied to another id is never changed. A checkout, portal or tying request without a valid session redirects to sign in first (and back), and Polar is never called. Held by `tests/unit/billing-subscribe.test.ts`, with a fake session for user A, a request carrying `external_customer_id`, `customer_id` and `customer_email` for user B in its query and body, and a fake Polar that records every call:
  - Polar only ever receives A's id and A's email, in every call;
  - an untied customer with A's email gets `update(id, { external_id: A })`, and the checkout's `customer_id` is that customer's;
  - an untied customer with A's email that already holds Pro is tied, then A goes to Account, with no checkout created;
  - a customer with A's email tied to B gets no update, no create and no checkout, and the conflict line shows;
  - with no customer at all, one is created with A's email and A's id, and the checkout is bound to it;
  - a soft deleted customer with A's email is ignored, and one is created;
  - a customer holding `A@X.com` while Clerk says `a@x.com` is tied;
  - two live matches give the conflict line, with no write;
  - a 422 on create lists once more and ties what it finds; a second 422 gives the checkout error line;
  - a tied customer with an active subscription to another product gets no checkout, and the other product line shows;
  - an unverified primary email makes no list, no create and no checkout;
  - with no session, the response is a redirect to `/sign-in` returning to the same page, and the fake Polar records no call.

  The session cases also cover `/account/billing`.
- **INV-4**: A visitor with no genuine Clerk token causes no outbound call. A forged or malformed token is refused locally.
- **INV-5**: No store, no cookie of our own, no webhook, no metadata written to Clerk. Polar is the only source of Pro. Our only writes to Polar are Subscribe's (tying a customer's external id, or creating a customer with the email and id), checkout, the portal session and deletion.
- **INV-6**: Pro is holding the RedactNest Pro benefit, never "any active subscription", because the Polar organisation will sell other products.
- **INV-7**: A Vercel production deploy runs only on live Clerk keys and Polar production, and nothing else runs on them. Every other Vercel deploy runs with billing off (AC-23).
- **INV-8**: Pricing and the legal pages promise only what Pro does today, and take every cap from `config`. The terms state no cap and no price as a number ("the price shown on Pricing when you subscribe").
- **INV-9**: Every word on `/tool` comes from `src/lib/flow-text.ts`, from a kind, the answer or frozen snapshot, and `config`. Every seller and agreement word comes from `src/lib/legal.ts`.
- **INV-10**: Every link to Pricing, an account page, Subscribe or Manage billing is a plain `a`, never `next/link`, so nothing prefetches a page that creates a checkout and `/tool` sends no request but the entitlement (spec 0002, AC-3).
- **INV-11**: "Genuine" means the signature, the issuer and the authorized party are all verified, and `nbf` and `iat` are not in the future. Only the expiry is relaxed, and only up to 7 days from `iat`. A token that fails any check gets the free limit with the "sign in again" plan line (`sign-in-needed`), and there is no Clerk server call on any path. Held by `tests/unit/billing-entitlement.test.ts`, signing tokens with a locally generated key pair: a bad signature, a wrong `iss`, a missing `azp`, a wrong `azp`, an `nbf` or `iat` in the future, and an `iat` 8 days ago, each on a fresh and on an expired token where it applies, all give free `sign-in-needed` with `__client_uat` both positive and absent, and the fake Polar records no call; a genuine expired token issued 6 days ago reaches Polar with its `sub`; and the test's Clerk backend client is a fake that fails the test if it is ever called.
- **INV-12**: The Vercel firewall rate limit rule's action is deny with a 429, never a challenge. A challenge sets Vercel's own cookie and, under spec 0011 INV-9, any firewall challenge mode changes the privacy policy first (claim C6). Held at go live (step 3's check): past the limit from one address, `/api/entitlement` answers 429 with no `Set-Cookie` and no challenge page.
- **INV-13**: Every way out of the `(account)` group is a full page load, so Clerk's script never outlives the account pages (claims C7 and C9). Links out are plain `a` elements or `Button` with `reload`; sign out and deletion end with `window.location.assign(HOME_PATH)` (AC-11, AC-12); and Clerk's own navigation only ever lands inside the group (AC-8). Lint bans `SignOutButton` and `UserButton` from `@clerk/nextjs` in every zone, because both sign out with Clerk's client side navigation. Held by `tests/unit/billing-landing.test.ts`, the sign out component test, `tests/unit/engine-wall.test.ts` for the ban, and the walk's check that `/` after sign out was a document load with no request to a Clerk origin.

**Security model**: accounts are an email and a session at Clerk; billing is Polar's. Signed in, a person acts on their own account only: every account page and action reads the user from Clerk's session (`auth()` through the proxy), never from a parameter. Subscribe may tie an existing Polar customer to the account only by an email Clerk has verified with an emailed code (AC-25), the same proof Polar's own portal asks for, so tying never hands anyone a customer they could not already open; a customer tied to another id is never touched. The tool's check trusts a token only when it is genuine (INV-11), and an expired one only within 7 days of issue. Secrets (`CLERK_SECRET_KEY`, `POLAR_ACCESS_TOKEN`) live in server only modules, are never `NEXT_PUBLIC_`, and are read only in `src/billing/clients.ts`. The entitlement grants nothing a visitor could not already take (spec 0001: the cap lives in code anyone can read and change), so its bar is "an honest visitor always gets the right answer, and nothing leaks", not "nobody can cheat". Card data never touches us: Polar's hosted checkout takes it (PCI DSS is Polar's and its processor's). The portal token Polar adds to the welcome address is a credential for that customer's billing, so the proxy drops it before Clerk or any page sees it (AC-26), and every route pins the referrer policy so no page's address reaches another origin (AC-27). **Compliance scope**: UK GDPR and EU GDPR for the account email and the sign in records (legal basis: the contract), with Clerk and Polar as our processors, each as its own DPA says. As merchant of record, Polar also uses billing details for fraud protection and security and keeps the records tax law requires, under its own privacy policy (the deferred lawyer review checks whether that part makes it a controller, Follow-up); consumer law on subscriptions (cancellation, notice of price changes, the right to withdraw), with Polar selling as merchant of record. **Audit**: Polar's and Clerk's records are the audit trail; we keep none (Decision). **Rate limits**: anonymous and forged traffic makes no outbound call (INV-4). A signed in abuser could spend the shared Polar limit (500 a minute) for every EdiventStudio product, so a Vercel firewall rate limit rule (deny with 429, never challenge, INV-12) on `/api/entitlement` and `/account/subscribe` is a required go live step, with the 10 s per user memo as the fallback if Pro offers no such rule.

## Consequences

**Positive**:
- Billing with no store: nothing to back up, migrate or breach, and "documents are never stored" stays structurally true.
- Pro is always current: a cancel, a refund or a lapse shows on the next answer, with no webhook to miss and no copy to drift. Sandbox testing needs no tunnel.
- `/tool` stays sealed: no Clerk or Polar code, no proxy, no new origin, the same single same origin request.
- A paying user is never quietly capped: every free answer carries its reason and a way forward, and every payment lands on a customer tied to the account the plan check looks up (AC-25).
- No new cookie, no outside script and no outside origin on the public pages, so most of the privacy policy's claims hold as they did.

**Negative / tradeoffs**:
- Every signed in check costs one Polar call, about a second at worst. A Polar outage shows Pro users "couldn't check your plan" and the free limit, except for 30 minutes after a confirmed Pro answer on the same page.
- The Polar rate limit (500 a minute) is shared with EdiventStudio's future products; the go live rate limit rule is what stops one abusive signed in account spending it for everyone.
- After a browser restart Clerk's session cookie is gone, so a Pro user going straight to `/tool` must click Sign in again once (the tab then picks it up on return).
- Clerk's free plan fixes sessions at 7 days, so a Pro user who stays on `/tool` sees "Sign in again" at least once a week, not once a month. The plan line says so out loud (AC-5); a longer window needs a paid Clerk plan and a new `SESSION_TRUST_MS`.
- A session revoked from another device, or by you in Clerk's dashboard, keeps Pro in this browser until its cookie goes (at most 7 days), because the tool's check never asks Clerk. Harmless while the cap is honesty based; revisit if Pro ever unlocks something server side.
- The route reads Clerk's cookies itself and relies on `verifyJwt` checking the signature before expiry. A Clerk change to either breaks rule 5 quietly to `sign-in-needed`; the pinned unit test catches the order, the decision table the cookie names.
- The paywall stays unenforceable (spec 0001): the cap lives in public code. Pro sells convenience and support, not a lock.
- Money is taken before the full lawyer review (deferred to 100+ users on a lawyer's advice). The subscriptions terms, refunds and withdrawal rights are drafted, not cleared.
- **Scan memory**: Pro lets a 50 page scan through, and blanking one match on every page of a 50 page grey scan measured 917 MB (scope Deferred). A tab that runs out of memory lands on the existing `lost` callout with nothing half written, so it fails safe, but a Pro user can meet it.
- Account deletion removes the whole Polar customer, which is right while RedactNest is EdiventStudio's only product with accounts, and wrong once one person buys two. Tying (AC-25) makes this reachable sooner: a tied customer who once bought another EdiventStudio product has that order history anonymised too when they delete their RedactNest account. A live subscription to another product blocks the deletion instead (AC-11). One whose payment is being retried does not: like Pro's, it never shows in the customer state, so the deletion goes ahead and Polar's delete cancels it.
- Subscribe creates the Polar customer before payment, so someone who leaves checkout keeps a Polar customer record (their email and account id) until they delete their account, and Account shows them Manage billing with nothing in it.
- An email tied to another account in Polar (for example a Clerk user removed by hand while their Polar customer stayed) cannot subscribe until you sort it out by hand, because Polar never lets an external id change. The conflict line asks them to write to you.
- A person who already subscribes to another EdiventStudio product with the same email cannot buy Pro, because the organisation allows one subscription per customer across its products. The other product line asks them to write to you; the lasting answer belongs to the customer identity Follow-up.
- Subscribe makes up to four Polar calls (the state, the list, the tie or create, the checkout) instead of two, and two more when a create meets a race. All on the account pages, none on `/tool`.
- Previews show no billing, so the first time billing runs on Vercel is production on go live day (step 7's real purchase). Locally, the sandbox run covers everything else.
- Clerk's prebuilt components bring Clerk's own markup to the account pages; their accessibility is Clerk's, checked by axe but not ours to fix.
- The first request back from Polar's checkout still carries `customer_session_token` to Vercel, and Vercel's request log keeps that line for the day its entry in `OUTSIDE_SERVICES` states ("the page or file asked for" already covers it, so no claim changes). The proxy's redirect (AC-26) makes it the only line, keeps it out of Clerk's handshake and out of the address bar, but nothing on our side can stop Polar putting it in that first address: no checkout field, setting or `success_url` placeholder turns it off (checked 4 October 2026 in the SDK's types and Polar's docs, `rationale.md`). Only the operator and Vercel can read that log, and there is no log drain (spec 0011).
- The proxy now does one more thing on every account page request: it reads the query before Clerk does. A request with the token costs one extra round trip.
- C6 names no cookie, so a visitor who wants the names must read their own browser's list. And no test can see a real Clerk instance's cookies, so the claim's signed in half rests on checks by hand: task 13's walk, Go live step 6, and a run after each Clerk upgrade (Follow-up).
- Naming Cloudflare ties C6 to Clerk's choice of network. If Clerk moves its host off Cloudflare, the sentence is stale until the next cookie check catches it.

**Neutral**:
- New dependencies `@clerk/nextjs` 7 and `@polar-sh/sdk` 1 (MIT), a new capability folder `src/billing`, a `src/proxy.ts`, an `(account)` route group, seven new configuration values and a billing off mode.
- The terms are renamed "Terms of service". Specs 0001, 0002, 0003, 0005, 0007, 0009 and 0011 are amended (task 17).
