**Reviewed by**: Sonnet 5.5 (author on Opus)
**Scope**: 45 files on the branch against main; the update since the third review (`c78357c..HEAD`, 18 files: footnote markers and glued labels, tasks 45 to 49) read in full, and `phone.ts`, `dates.ts`, `text.ts`, `src/engine/find.ts` read where they bear on it
**Verdict**: Approve with nits

## Summary
The update does what it says and I could not break it where it matters most. A differential fuzz of the base (`c78357c`) against HEAD over about 1.7 million generated layouts (cards, Social Security numbers and dates, each with realistic labels, digit groups, punctuation, footnote markers and other values beside them) found no value that lost a row, no row that lost its tick, and no card that passes on its own digits and now starts unticked. I also ran a real 8 point raised `1` through the engine's own find step on real MuPDF, and it behaves as the spec assumes (glued at gaps up to 1 pt, so the date, card and number are listed with the marker outside the row). No Blocker or Major. The headline is a gap in the update's own promise: a dotted, hyphenated or ISO date with a marker, when a digit group sits beside it on the line, is read by the phone detector as a phone number, so the date of birth is listed under Phone, unticked, with the neighbour's digits inside the row (INV-14 no longer holds for it). The rest are Minor and nits, mostly about forms the update left on the dropped side with a measured cost to move them.

## Minor
### 🟡 A date with a marker is taken by the phone detector when a digit group is beside it, `src/detect/phone.ts:344`, `src/detect/dates.ts:46`
**Problem**: The phone detector keeps off a date only when a whole unit is a numeric date (`isNumericDate`, INV-14). With a marker the unit is `05.12.19801`, which is no date, so a phone window forms across it and its digit-group neighbour, and the phone row (which wins by `PRECEDENCE`) takes the date. Through the real engine, with a real raised `1` (8 pt, gap 0) after `John Smith 05.12.1980` and `44 Engineer` drawn after it on the same line, `findMatches` returns `phone-[05.12.19801 44]` (unticked), with `Born 05.12.1980¹ 44 years` the same: no date row, no tick for the birth word, and the age inside the row. The same for the hyphen and ISO forms (`John Smith 1980-12-05¹ 44` gives `phone-[1980-12-051 44]`), for `Page 3 05.12.1980¹` and `12 05.12.1980¹` (a group before it), and for `27-09-2026¹ 5` (ticked, as a phone row). The slash form (`12/05/1980¹ 44`), the written forms, the card and the Social Security number are not affected (the phone grammar does not join across a slash or a month name). Reproduced through `detect`: `DOB: 05.12.1980¹ 12 High Street` gives `phone-[05.12.19801 12]`, where the same text without the marker gives `date+[05.12.1980]`.
**Why it matters**: Not a silent miss: a row exists, and the base gave the same phone row. But the update's headline is "a date of birth with a reference mark is listed and ticked" (AC-19, Consequences), and for these layouts it is listed under the wrong label, unticked despite the birth word, and wider than the date (ticking it removes the neighbour's digits too). A dotted date followed by a number is a plain table row. INV-14 ("a full numeric date is never read as a phone number") is now false for a date the date detector itself reads, and none of the through-`detect` tests has a digit group beside a marked date, so the new "with no phone row" test (`tests/unit/detect.test.ts:253`, which claims INV-14) cannot see it.
**Suggested fix**: Let the phone unit mark ask the same question the date detector answers: a unit that is a numeric date with a four digit year plus exactly one ASCII digit is a date unit (add a small `isNumericDateWithMarker` beside `isNumericDate` and use it at `phone.ts:344`). Measured on a scratch copy: 0 of 1,102 test strings and fixture lines change through `detect`; the four examples above become `date-[05.12.1980]`, `date+[05.12.1980]` (with a birth word), `date-[1980-12-05]` and `date-[27-09-2026]`; the phone side loses only a phone unit shaped like a real date plus one digit (`12-05-19801`), about 0.1% of random `NN-NN-NNNNN` units by arithmetic, and those become a date row and a leftover digit. Add through-`detect` cases with a number before and after a dotted, hyphen and ISO date.

### 🟡 One footnote digit after a two digit year still gives no row, `src/detect/date.ts:459`
**Problem**: `markerAt` requires a four digit year. `Born 12/05/80¹` (`12/05/801`), `05.12.80¹` and `DOB: 5.12.80²` give no row of any kind, through `detect` and through the engine with a real raised marker. Every unexplained miss in a 140,800 cell matrix (25 prefixes, 8 markers, 44 suffixes, 16 forms) was a two digit year form with a marker; all 123,200 cells with a four digit year were covered.
**Why it matters**: The reason recorded (`05.12.801` could be `05.12.80` and a marker or `05.12.8` and `01`) is sound for a one digit month or day, and the spec lists the case as a decision. But a day first or month first date with two digit groups and a two digit year (`12/05/80`) is how a date of birth is often typed on US and UK forms, the owner's rule is "extra rows are fine, missed values are not", and the cost is small once the shape is narrowed. Measured on a scratch copy that allows the marker after a two digit year only when both leading groups have two digits: random `NN/NN/NNN`, `NN-NN-NNN` and `NN.NN.NNN` codes list an unticked date 6.4% to 6.7% of the time (4,000 samples each), while `N.N.NNN` and `N/N/NNN` (version numbers: 81% if every two digit year were allowed) and `N.NN.NNN` stay at 0.0%. Of 1,102 test strings and fixture lines two change, both pins (`05.12.80¹`, and `05.12.198`, which would list `05.12.19`).
**Suggested fix**: Either allow it for two digit groups only, as measured, and flip those two pins, or keep the drop and add `12/05/80¹` to the *Consequences* list next to `05.12.80¹` with this cost so the choice is on the page. I would take the first.

### 🟡 A bare Social Security number next to an SSN word, with a marker, is still dropped, `src/detect/us-ssn.ts:116`
**Problem**: `SSN 123456789¹` (and `SSN: 123456789¹`) gives no row. The marker tolerance requires a separated number (`separator !== null`), on the reason that "only an SSN word and its length of nine say what a bare run is". But the SSN word is exactly what the bare path already requires, so the word and the nine digits are both there. A labelled bare number is how exports and CSV-like tables write one. (`Social Security number: 345678901¹` is listed only by accident, as the unticked phone row `3456789011`.)
**Why it matters**: A labelled Social Security number is the value this detector exists for. It is dropped with no row, and the footnote layout is rare, so Minor. The cost of reading it is almost nil: the number must already follow an SSN word, so only a ten digit run right after the word changes.
**Suggested fix**: Drop the `separator !== null` condition so a bare run after an SSN word with exactly one trailing ASCII digit lists unticked. Measured on a scratch copy: 3 of 1,102 test strings change (`SSN 123456789¹`, `SSN 1234567890`, `SSN 1234567891`, all pins); a random ten digit run after `SSN ` lists an unticked row 89.1% of the time (the SSA's pass rate), which is the same figure the spec accepts for the separated shape and applies only to a ten digit run directly after an SSN word.

### 🟡 The premise of the whole update, a real superscript reads as glued, is held by no test, `scripts/lib/detection-fixtures.mjs:445`, `tests/unit/detection.test.ts`
**Problem**: Every footnote fixture line draws the marker as a plain `1` at 12 pt on the baseline (`Born 12/05/19811 per the register`), and the unit tests build the glued text by NFKC. Nothing in the repository passes a smaller, raised glyph through MuPDF and the find step. The third review measured it with `asText()`; I repeated it through `openDocumentWith` and `findMatches` (Helvetica 12 pt, marker at `Tm` +5 pt): at 8 pt and 7 pt the marker is glued up to a gap of 1.0 pt, at 6.5 pt up to 0.5 pt, and `after` reads `1`; at 1.2 pt it is ` 1`. Date, card (spaced, hyphenated, unbroken, Amex) and Social Security number all come back as the spec says, the marker outside the row.
**Why it matters**: `mupdf` is pinned exact, but an upgrade is a deliberate event and the notices test already guards it; if a later MuPDF started to put a raised glyph on its own line, nothing here would fail while every marker silently went back to giving no row (a spaced marker is fine, a glued one is the miss). The fixture claim "each marker drawn as a plain glued `1`" is honest, but it means the feature's one empirical premise is a review note, not a test.
**Suggested fix**: One line in a detection fixture or in `detection.test.ts`'s `onePage` helper, with the marker as a second `BT` at 8 pt and a `Tm` rise of about 5 pt, and an assertion that `findMatches` returns the date, tick and text without the marker.

## Nits
- ⚪ `src/detect/date.ts:116`, `DATE_LABELS` misses common glued labels, which the spec records as "a label found missing is a word to add". Measured missing today (each lists nothing): `Created27/09/2026`, `Updated27/09/2026`, `Modified27/09/2026`, `Printed27/09/2026`, `Due27/09/2026`, `Deadline27/09/2026`, `Starts27/09/2026`, `Ends31/12/2026`, `Date of issue27/09/2026`. A word costs nothing unless a real code spells it (the random-letter figures are 0.1% to 0.4% for the whole list), so these are free to add; I would add `created`, `updated`, `modified`, `printed`, `due`, `deadline`, `start`, `starts`, `end`, `ends`, `issue`.
- ⚪ `src/detect/date.ts:441`, the asymmetry raised by `/test`, confirmed: `cutAtEnd` returns on a marker before it looks at the own separator, so the detector alone lists `27-09-20261-5`, `2026-09-271-5` and `27/09/20261/5` while `2026-09-27-1` and `27-09-2026-1` give nothing. Through `detect` the first two are covered by a phone row (`phone+[27-09-20261-5]`), so nothing is lost and no extra date row shows; only the slash form lists an unticked date. Harmless, but the header comment says a hyphen or slash and one digit is "the shape of a build number"; pin the marker-then-separator case either way.
- ⚪ `src/detect/card.ts:262`, the second `/test` note, confirmed: `Card 4111 1111 1111 11111١` lists the card unticked (the unit stops at the ASCII digits, so the Arabic-Indic digit is outside it), while the date and Social Security rules drop a marker followed by another script's digit. - ⚪ A footnoted card sometimes leaves the marker as a one digit phone row (`5500 0000 0000 0004¹ 123` gives `card-[...]` and `phone-[1]`), the card analogue of the recorded `SSN 223-45-6789¹` piece. - ⚪ `tests/unit/detect.test.ts:242`, a doc comment is on the wrong test: "Pinned as a known behaviour ... the marker is left as a phone piece" sits directly above the "other forms" `it.each`, and the test it describes (`leaves the marker after SSN 223-45-6789¹ as a ticked phone piece`, line 273) has none.
- ⚪ The pin count disagrees across the docs: `docs/scope/scope.md:278` says "the six pinned misses flipped", `verify.md` "the eight flipped pins", the spec "eight results change, seven of them pinned misses". Pick one. And the scope line `Review it (fresh model)` is ticked `[x]` while saying "owed again for the third update".

## Strengths
- The update is general in the way the owner asked: one digit, ASCII, nothing after it, a four digit year, and every exemption can only add a row. My 1.7 million layout differential found no third rejection hiding in the rewrite of `cutAtStart` and none lost to the dot lift.
- The card design is careful where it could easily have gone wrong: a passing window beats a tolerated one at the same unit, a row is ticked when any window inside it passes whole (the cross check's `4521 …` case), and the hyphen group of one is kept out. I could not find a real card that starts unticked or loses a digit, with a marker or without, in 380,000 layouts.
- The tests name each rule and each kept rejection, the card test regenerates the spec's windows from its own Luhn and compares row edges to group edges (with markers allowed), the fixtures regenerate byte for byte, and the costs are measured and written down before the code (rationale tables), which made every figure above easy to cross check.

## Test coverage
Strong for the rule as written: 878 tests across the five changed files pass, the adversarial file (1,202) passes alone within budget, the engine tests read the three new fixture lines through real MuPDF quads, and the card block checks every passing and tolerated window in seeded runs. The gaps are the ones above: no through-`detect` case has a digit group beside a marked dotted, hyphen or ISO date (so INV-14's phone guard is untested for it), no fixture or test uses a raised, smaller marker, a marker after a card and then a non-ASCII digit is unpinned, and the marker-then-own-separator case (`27-09-20261-5`) is unpinned.
