# Third review, feat/remaining-detectors, 2026-10-10

**Reviewed by**: Sonnet 5.5 (author on Opus)
**Scope**: 44 files on the branch against main; the update since the second review (`1a45d46..HEAD`, 10 files, the general date boundary rule) read in full, the rest only where it bears on it
**Verdict**: Changes requested

## Summary
The general boundary rule does what it says. I probed it with about 640,000 generated layouts (a 200,000 run realistic-neighbour fuzz, a 400,000 run broad-alphabet fuzz with an oracle for the five stated rejections, and a 40,176 cell interval grid) and found no date lost outside the rejections the spec names. The interval, time, offset, zone, birth-word and month-first range cases all hold, and the date detector stays linear. One real silent miss remains, in the family the update set out to close: a footnote marker set flush against a date (`Born 12/05/1980¹`, which NFKC reads as `12/05/19801`) gives no row at all, including for a date of birth. It is the one case where the owner's rule ("a full real date is never dropped for what follows it") is not kept, the same reasoning was already accepted for IBANs, and I confirmed with real MuPDF text extraction that a flush marker does glue. The rest is minor: the same footnote miss for a card and a Social Security number, label-and-separator prefixes (`Exp.31.12.2026`), some date forms that are not on the recorded list, and one dead line.

## Major
### 🟠 A footnote marker (or any one digit) flush after a full date gives no row, `src/detect/date.ts:363`, `src/detect/date.ts:415`
**Problem**: A numeric date reads each group up to 4 digits and returns `null` at a fifth (line 415 to 416). A written date's end check drops it when a digit touches the year (`cutAtEnd`, line 363). NFKC turns a superscript `¹ ² ³` into `1 2 3`, so a date followed by a footnote reference becomes a date with a five digit year, which is not a date. Header comment lines 52 to 53 say so ("A digit glued to either end is never a date at all"), and four tests pin it (`tests/unit/detect-dates.test.ts:165`, `:166`, `:187`, `:601`, plus `:797` for another script's digits).

Failing inputs, run through the real `detect` (all after NFKC), each giving no row of any kind:
- `Born 12/05/1980¹` (`Born 12/05/19801`): the date of birth, no row, no warning.
- `Signed 27 September 2026¹`, `Sep 27 2026²`, `1st of May 2026³`.
- `2026-09-27¹` (`2026-09-271`).
- All 20 date forms in my matrix, with `¹`, `²`, `³` or a bare `1`, `12`, `123` after them, with and without a `DOB:` prefix.
- A dotted date at a sentence end with a marker after the full stop, `geboren am 12.05.1980.¹` (`12.05.1980.1`): the date's own separator then a digit.

Is the marker really flush? I built a one-page PDF (Helvetica 12 pt, `Born 12/05/1980`, then a raised `1` at 8 pt or 6.5 pt) and read it back with MuPDF's structured text, as the engine does. With the marker drawn at the end of the text (gap 0) MuPDF returns `Born 12/05/19801`. The same holds for a gap of 0.3, 0.6 and 1.0 pt at 8 pt; at 6.5 pt a gap of 1 pt already gives `Born 12/05/1980 1`. A word processor sets a reference mark directly after the text, so gap 0 is the normal case. The written form gives `Born 27 September 20261`, and the dotted form `Geboren am 12.05.1980.1`.

**Why it matters**: This is the same silent miss the last two rounds were about, and the one place the owner's stated rule is not kept ("a full real date is never dropped for what follows it; keep rejecting only what measurement shows must be rejected"). The rationale's evidence has no measurement for a glued digit: its tables measure the own separator, the letter before, and the file name and code shapes, but not a digit after a year. The spec's INV-17 also contradicts itself: it says a date "is dropped only for a letter before the separator before it, or for its own separator running on with digits", while AC-19 and the code also drop it for a glued digit. The footnote reading was already accepted for IBANs (AC-21 lists `GB82...32¹` ticked) and for National Insurance numbers with a suffix. A date of birth is the value this detector exists for; with a birth word before it, the row would also be ticked, and today it simply is not there.

**Suggested fix**: Read a date when exactly one ASCII digit follows it flush, and leave that digit outside the row (listed unticked unless a birth word ticks it, as for every other glued date). In `numericAt`, accept a last group of five digits whose first four are a real year (and, for the year first form, a last group of three digits whose first two are a real day), and in `writtenAt` accept one digit after the year; keep two or more digits dropped, and keep non-ASCII digits dropped (the `١` pins stay). For the dotted sentence-end form, the same single digit after the own separator when the year has four digits. Flip the four pins and the INV-17 and AC-19 sentences with it.

I measured the cost on a scratch copy of `src/detect/date.ts` (prototype for the two numeric cases and the written case, not the dotted one): random codes of the shape `N/N/NNNNN` list a date 1.8% of the time (4,000 samples), `20NN-NN-NNN` 3.7%, `NN.NN.NNNNN` 0.1%, `NNNN-NN-NNN` 0.1%, and `NN-NN-NNNNN`, `NN-NN-NNNNNN`, `NNN-NN-NNNNN` 0.0%. Across 912 test strings and fixture lines I extracted from `tests/unit/detect*.test.ts` and `scripts/lib/detection-fixtures.mjs`, only six change, all of them the pinned digit-glued cases (`05.12.19800`, `on 1 May 20261 we`, `on 05.12.19801 we`, `27 September 20261`, `27 September 2026١` (the prototype admitted any Unicode digit, the fix should admit ASCII only), and the second date of `05-12-1980-3 June 20261`). That is far under the noise the author already accepted for dated codes (100% on several shapes, listed unticked). It also removes the 27/09/20261-28/09/2026 case where the first date of an interval is lost.

## Minor
### 🟡 The same footnote marker after a card or a Social Security number gives no row, `src/detect/us-ssn.ts:90`, `src/detect/card.ts:209`
**Problem**: The IBAN fix reads past a glued end because its length and checksum say where it stops. A card and a Social Security number were left out. `SSN 123-45-6789¹` and `SSN 123 45 6789¹` (`...67891`) give no row, because `partsAt` returns `null` when a digit follows (line 90). `Card 4111 1111 1111 1111¹`, `Card 4111-1111-1111-1111¹` and `Card 4111111111111111¹` give no row either: the marker joins the last unit (`11111`, 17 digits), and no window of whole units passes. `SSN 123-45-6789.`, `...a`, `Card 4111 1111 1111 1111.`, `...a` and `... ¹` (with a space) all work.
**Why it matters**: A card or a Social Security number with a reference mark after it is less common than a footnoted date, but it is the same cause, the harm of a missed value is larger, and the author already decided for the IBAN that the marker must not hide it. `AB123456¹` (a National Insurance number with no suffix) is the recorded accepted miss; the card and the Social Security number are not on any recorded list.
**Suggested fix**: Apply the National Insurance stance, not the IBAN one (no checksum on the SSN): list the value with one glued digit unticked. For a card, when the last unit has one digit too many and the window without that digit passes Luhn and the brand table, list the window unticked. I measured the false-positive share at 2.63% of random 17 digit strings (100,000 samples) for the card, and 88.9% for `NNN-NN-NNNN` random digits passing the SSA's rules, so the Social Security case only fits as unticked, and only for exactly one glued digit. If the owner prefers the current rule, record both in *Consequences* next to `AB123456¹`.

### 🟡 A word or label glued to a date by a dot, hyphen or slash drops it, `src/detect/date.ts:343`
**Problem**: The kept letter-before-the-separator rejection (line 343) was measured against `REF-` and `INV-` codes, file names and URL paths, all with a hyphen or slash. A short label that ends in a full stop and runs into its value, which is how abbreviations are set, is the same shape and is not measured. Each of these gives no row (values real, all numeric forms): `Exp.31.12.2026`, `Dt.27/09/2026`, `Date.31.12.2026`, `b.12/05/1980`, `Date-27/09/2026`, `Valid-31/12/2026`, `M/12/05/1980`, `M-12/05/1980`, `Monday-28/09/2026`, `27/09/2026 Mon-28/09/2026` (second date lost), `27/09/2026 noon-28/09/2026`.
**Why it matters**: `Smith-12/05/1980` was accepted on purpose, and `REF-05.12.1980` is the code shape the rule exists for. A label such as `Dt.`, `Exp.` or `Date-` is not a code, and the date after it is a real date with no row and no warning. The glued birth word already gets this exemption (`D.O.B.12.05.1980`), so the principle is in place for one word list. I rate it Minor because I cannot say how common a glued label is in a PDF's text; the spec itself says it was not measured ("How often a PDF's text puts a word right against the start of a value was not measured"), and the dot shape (`XXX.DD.MM.YYYY`) is not in the rationale's random shape table, so I cannot separate it from `report.27.09.2026.pdf` by shape alone.
**Suggested fix**: Either extend the birth word exemption to a short list of date labels (`date`, `dt`, `dated`, `exp`, `expiry`, `issued`, `valid`, `from`, `until`), listed unticked, or measure the dot case and say in *Consequences* that a label glued to a date is a known miss. Contrived cases (`noon-`, `Mon-`) I would leave and record.

### 🟡 Date forms that are not read and not on the recorded list, `src/detect/dates.ts:30`, `src/detect/date.ts:177`
**Problem**: The known list (`05-Jan-2026`, `27/Sep/2026`, `2026.09.27`, `20260927`, `27 Sep 26`, `15 March,2026`) is recorded. These real dates also give no row and are not on it: `2026/9/7` and `2026-9-7` (year first with one digit month or day, as Excel and East Asian exports write them), `27 / 09 / 2026` and `27 . 09 . 2026` (spaced separators), `27th day of September 2026` and `Dated this 1st day of May, 2026` (the legal formula), `27. September 2026`, `September, 27 2026` and `Sept.27, 2026`. `YEAR_FIRST` in `dates.ts` is `[0-9]{4}` then two digit month and day only.
**Why it matters**: Not boundary misses, so outside this round's rule, but the Follow-up item "bring to /architect the date forms the detector has no pattern for" would be incomplete without them, and `2026/9/7` and the day-of-month formula are common.
**Suggested fix**: Add them to the *Consequences* list and the Follow-up. `isNumericDate` is shared with the phone detector (INV-14), so `2026/9/7` needs the phone look-alike cases rerun, as the existing Follow-up says for `2026.09.27`.

## Nits
- ⚪ `src/detect/date.ts:273`, the `|| birthGluedTo(points, at)` on the tick is dead code and its comment is wrong. `wordBefore` slices the window up to `at`, so the date's own digits are never in it, and the "whole word test refuses a word with a digit after it" does not happen. Removing the call changes nothing: `Patient DOB27/09/1980`, `DOB-12/05/1980`, `D.O.B.12.05.1980`, `Born-05.12.1980`, `DOB27 September 1980`, `date of birth12/05/1980`, `birthdate12/05/1980` and `born05.12.1980` stay ticked with it removed (scratch copy), and `Reborn27/09/1980` and `xBorn-05.12.1980` stay unlisted. The uses of `birthGluedTo` in `gluedAt` and `cutAtStart` are needed. The spec's reason for the tick rule and the rationale row "The tick of a date with something glued after it" should drop the claim. (Noted by the engineer; confirmed.)
- ⚪ `src/detect/date.ts:346`, the time stretch lifts both start rejections for any stretch of digits, spaces, `: . , + @`, hyphens and short letter runs, so a date shortly before a code can read a date out of the code. Confirmed as the engineer said: `Paid 27/09/2026 ref 1/05/12/1980` lists `05/12/1980` and `Leave 2026-09-01-2026-09-30-01` lists `09-30-01` (and drops `2026-09-30`, which is part of the code). - ⚪ `docs/specs/0005-pattern-detection/index.md:282` (INV-17), the sentence "it is dropped only for a letter before the separator before it, or for its own separator running on with digits on either side" omits the glued digit and the glued letter at the start. See the Major.

## Checks you asked for
- **Full real dates dropped by what follows**: only a glued digit (the Major) and the date's own separator with a digit (`2026-09-27-01`, `05.12.1980.17`, `1/05/12/1980`, kept on purpose; the dotted sentence end with a marker is in the Major). Letters, words, punctuation, brackets, quotes, currency, times, zones, ordinals (`27ᵗʰ`, `1ˢᵗ` through NFKC), `*`, `†`, `‡`, `§`, other dates, `/` + word, `-` + word, table cells (`John|12/05/1980|M`, `John,12/05/1980,M`, tab separated, `12/05/1980M`) all keep the date.
- **Kept rejection 1 (letter before the date or its separator)**: drops real values only where a label or word is glued, see the Minor above. A letter glued right before (`Dated27 September 2026`, `M12/05/1980`, `Smith12/05/1980M`) is dropped as designed, and an unmeasured share of real layouts.
- **Kept rejection 2 (own separator with a digit)**: real layouts that hit it are the dotted sentence end plus a footnote (`12.05.1980.¹`), a date then `-` and a time (`2026-09-27-10:30`, rare) and a slash date then `/1` (`27/09/2026/1`, rare). `01-05-1980-31-05-1980`, `05-12-1980-3 June 2026` and every chain of full dates are exempt and found.
- **Interval and time cases**: all forms in AC-19 and the 40,176 cell grid (8 first dates, 62 times and trailers, 9 joiners, 9 second dates) lose a date only when a word stands directly before the joiner (`noon-`, `midnight-`, `Mon-`, `Monday-`, `inclusive-`, `approx-`, and `#3-` before a hyphen dated second date), which is the recorded six letter or no digit limit, or when a footnote digit touches the first date. `27/09/2026 10:00-28/09/2026 11:00`, the offset forms, the zone names, `@ 10:00`, `10h00`, `UTC+1` and `PM EST` all give both dates.
- **Birth word glue**: `DOB27/09/1980`, `DOB-12/05/1980`, `D.O.B.12.05.1980`, `Born-05.12.1980`, `DOB27 September 1980` are listed ticked; `Reborn27/09/1980` is not.
- **Card, IBAN, Social Security, National Insurance**: in a 150,000 run fuzz (31 value shapes, 42 prefixes, 43 suffixes), no value was left without a row except a card followed by `-12` or `/12` (hyphen or slash glued short group, in the spec's Card neighbours list) and the footnote cases in the Minor above. IBAN with a footnote, a word, or a digit after it keeps its exact length row; `IBANGB82...` (glued before) is the recorded one sided fix; `NI AB123456C¹` is listed unticked; `NI AB123456¹` is the recorded accepted miss.
- **Linear time**: 17 shapes (labels before, own separator before, joiner chains, spaced ranges, glued birth words, long times, times after times, digits glued) at 100,000 and 400,000 characters through the date detector: 25 to 80 ms and 99 to 310 ms, a ratio of 3.5 to 4.1 for a 4 times larger input. No quadratic read.
- **Fixtures**: `detect-date.pdf` and the other four detect fixtures regenerate byte for byte from `scripts/lib/detection-fixtures.mjs` (checked in a scratch script, nothing written). The engine tests read them through real MuPDF.
- **Wall and conventions**: no console, storage, network or `any` in `src/detect/date.ts`; constants (`TIME_LONGEST`, `TIME_LETTERS_MOST`, `DAY_RANGE`, `SPACED_DAY_RANGE`) are commented as pattern rules and live in `src/detect`; `search()` and `findNumbers` are not used. ESLint and Prettier are clean on the five changed code and test files.

## Strengths
- The rule is general in the way the owner asked: two named rejections, each with a measured reason, and every exemption (joiner, time, glued birth word) can only add a row. My 400,000 run oracle fuzz found no third rejection hiding in the code.
- The time stretch judged by characters rather than parsed is the right call: no grammar to miss a form, the 40 code point cap is held both ways in tests, and it stays linear on every shape I tried.
- The tests name each kept rejection (`finds no date with %s`) and each exemption separately, with the exact row text and tick, so widening a rule is noticed. The edges block holds `TIME_LONGEST` and `TIME_LETTERS_MOST` at their exact values.
- The fixture, its `verify.md` step and the engine test hold the new rows through real MuPDF quads, and the fixtures regenerate byte for byte.

## Test coverage
Strong for the rule as written: the interval, time, offset, zone, birth word, spaced range and every kept rejection have unit tests, a fixture and an engine check, and the adversarial file gains the shapes the new reads make (all pass alone). The gaps are the cases above: nothing pins a footnote marker after a date, a card or a Social Security number as a deliberate miss or as found (the only pins are the digit glued ones that say "no row"), nothing pins a label glued to a date with a full stop, and the dead tick call has no test that would have shown it dead.
